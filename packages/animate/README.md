# @drawcall/animate

Human motion for three.js, written down as what the body does.

A `Body` is a person as joints that turn, which turn the bones of a rigged humanoid. A motion is a story that says what must hold: where a foot, a hand or the hips are to be, and how they are moving there, how a hand is turned and how its fingers are held. The rest comes from the body:

- **Every part has a strength.** It takes its time when there is time, draws back first if it has to arrive fast, and swings on afterwards.
- **Only the ground pushes.** The hips are given a place to go and a speed to go at, and speed up and slow down only while a foot is on the ground: with none down they go on as they are going, keeping their height between strides. The feet step under them, a stride on from the last footfall, further ahead of the hips the harder they are braked, and he sinks and leans into it. At a walk a foot is always down, at a run both are in the air between strides, and off the ground he flies as he was going.
- **Joints turn only as far as joints do.**
- **What is not written reacts.** The strides come out even onto a foot that is to be planted, and he leaps onto it off a foot that is to kick; the trunk leans into a push, against a leg swung out, and swings into a fast hand; it bends, with the hips coming down, for a hand that would not reach otherwise; the arms swing against the legs; the pelvis turns with the feet, a little in a stride and side-on between feet the story has put apart.
- **He goes and faces any way.** He turns to go where he is sent, on the spot before he sets off and round a curve his strides can make once he is going, head and trunk first, and slows for a turn he could not make otherwise. He faces the way he goes unless a story says which way he faces: then he walks on with his pelvis turned, sidesteps or backs, by how far off the way he goes that is. A place within a stride of him he steps to as he faces.
- **Asking too much is an error** that says which part cannot do what, and what would help.

```ts
import { Body, type Story } from '@drawcall/animate'

function* shot(body: Body, ball: Ball): Story {
  const spot = ball.position.z
  // the left foot comes down beside the ball and a little behind it, he brakes onto it, and the right foot meets the ball
  body.run({ speed: 3.6, plant: 'left', at: spot - 0.35, arrive: 2.5 })
  const through = meeting(ball, 20, degrees(8.6))
  yield* body.foot.right.to(through.at, through)
  strike(ball, body.foot.right.value, body.foot.right.velocity)

  yield* body.stand()
}

const body = new Body(rig)
scene.add(rig)
body.play(shot(body, ball))
renderer.setAnimationLoop(() => {
  body.update(timer.getDelta())
  renderer.render(scene, camera)
})
```

`rig` is the rigged character, a three.js `Object3D` with its bones under it; `meeting`, `strike` and `degrees` are the example's own: where a foot meets the ball, what the ball does then, and degrees as radians. The strides and how they come out onto the planted foot, the moment of the strike, the leap onto the planted foot, the backswing, the lean back, the follow-through and the steps that bring him to a stand are not in the story.

## The rig

`new Body(rig)` takes a humanoid rigged with [`@drawcall/rig`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@drawcall/rig), the `scene` its loader gives or the object given to its `skin()`, and from then on turns its bones. It finds them by the names a body asks of a rig: `hips`, `spine`, `head`, and on each side `leftUpperLeg`, `leftLowerLeg`, `leftFoot`, `leftUpperArm`, `leftLowerArm`, `leftHand`; one that is missing, or does not hang from the one before it, is an error. A hand may have fingers: `leftThumbMetacarpal`, `leftThumbProximal`, `leftThumbDistal`, and `leftIndexProximal`, `leftIndexIntermediate`, `leftIndexDistal`, with `Middle`, `Ring` and `Little` named like the index: each finger three bones from the knuckle out, hanging from one another, and one bone hanging from its last where the fingertip is, as a skeleton for `@drawcall/rig` ends every chain. A hand has all five or none; with none, whatever a story says of fingers is an error. Any other bone stays as it is against the bone it hangs from. The rig faces +z, its left at +x, and stands on y = 0, in metres, and the pose it is given in is its rest pose, arms out or half down, the elbows straight or a little bent. A hand with fingers may rest bent at the wrist, its forearm turned any way and its fingers curled: its knuckles tell how it lies, and it is counted in line with its forearm, the palm facing the thigh were the arm let down. One without fingers is counted as the rig has it, which is then to be in line with the forearm and palm down with the arm out. Its ankles are above the ground and lower than the ball of a foot is ahead of them, 0.182 of the leg: boots walk, stilts are an error. Nothing in a rig tells how it stood before it was posed, so a body is made of a rig at rest, and one that plainly is not, turned round, off the ground, its trunk not upright or a leg not straight down, is an error; a rig in other units than metres cannot be told, and gives a body of that size.

`body.size` is what the body measures there, between its joints, in the lengths of what holds the rig, so that the rig's own `scale` counts: scaled to half, it is a body half as large. Everything it does is a share of that: `size.stand` is how high the hips stand, `size.ankle` how high an ankle is above the ground it stands on, `size.hip` how far out from the middle a hip is, `size.leg` and `size.arm` how long a leg is from hip to ankle and an arm from shoulder to wrist. Its times and speeds follow from the leg and gravity: `size.beat` is how long the leg, hanging, takes over a radian of its swing, which is 2π there and back (0.3 s for a man), and `size.pace` how fast that is over its length (2.9 m/s). A body of another size makes the same motion in its own lengths and its own time, so a story that is to play on any rig counts its places in `size.leg` or `size.stand`, its speeds in `size.pace` and its moments in `size.beat`; one written in metres and seconds is for the size it was written for. The bones go far from where the mesh was bound, so a skinned mesh wants `frustumCulled = false`.

## Writing a story

A story is a generator. A statement takes effect at once; `yield*` waits for the story it returns. `body.play` can be given several stories, which then go on side by side. What a story has stated stays in force when it ends, as does what is stated with no story playing: he comes to a stand when told to `stand`. Moments are seconds on the body's clock, `body.time`; `yield* body.until(moment)` waits for one, `body.when(place)` tells the moment the hips come by a place on the ground that is on their way, going as they are, or to rest where a walk ends, and `body.highest()` the moment he is highest in the jump he is making, or in the air, is to make next.

Places are `{ x, y, z }` in metres (a three.js `Vector3` will do), counted from where the rig is, along its axes: y up, and he starts out facing +z, his left at +x; put the rig anywhere in a scene. A place on the ground is `{ x, z }`, and a number where one is asked for is that far along z on x = 0. What is said in places, a foot or a hand sent somewhere, a `palm`, a velocity, is in the scene whichever way he faces; what is `on` him, and `body.ground`, goes with the way he faces. Standing, the hips are `body.size.stand` high and an ankle `body.size.ankle` above the ground it stands on: `body.ground(side, place)` is where a foot stands so, under its hip, he over that place. `body.facing` is the way he faces, a direction on the ground. `body.front(side, up, inward)` is how far ahead of a shoulder his front is, his skin or what he wears, so far above that shoulder and so far in from it toward his middle: where what he holds rests against his chest or in his shoulder. `foot.anchor` is where the ground holds a foot, if it does, and `body.playing` whether a story still is. A foot or a hand is planned when it is sent: out of reach of where it is sent, it is there at the first moment the body, going as it is, brings it near enough, and if that never comes it is an error at the statement. In the air both feet are spare, for as long as he is, and a foot sent somewhere there is the body's again when he comes down: a foot that is to arrive there leaves the ground no sooner than his last stride is made. A walk or a run stated in the air he lands in, on the foot that has been off the ground longer, the other stepping on. For a hand sent lower than it reaches he bends, and comes down as far as is still needed, in time for the moment and no sooner (going along, no lower than a lunge: lower he gets only standing with his feet together, and he comes up again before he steps off), and for a foot sent further along the ground than he stands the hips come down. Sent to rest somewhere, a hand or a foot stays at that place, whatever the rest of him does, until it is sent elsewhere or let `free`; one he takes out of reach of it is an error. A foot or a hand can instead be sent to a place on the body, counted from its hip or shoulder, where it then stays; the velocity it arrives with is always how it is moving in the scene: `hand.to(place, { on: body })`. The trunk, the head, the arms and forearms (`body.trunk`, `body.head`, `body.arm.left`, `body.forearm.left`) are sent to rotations, `{ x, y, z }` in rad about those axes, against what each hangs on: x leans the trunk forward and swings an arm back. With `seconds`, a foot or a hand sets off at once, or as soon as what it was sent before is done, and takes that long; without, it takes its time.

A place can instead be on a three.js `Object3D` in his scene, counted in its frame: `hand.to(place, { on: thing })`. A hand, foot or fingertip sent there arrives and then stays on it, moving as it moves, until sent elsewhere or let `free`, and a `palm` or `pointing` is then counted in the object's frame. A thing moves between updates, so the scene moves it after an update, for the frame it draws: the limb goes on with it as it was seen to move, from where the limb starts, and follows it as hard as its joints turn. One that cannot stay, out of reach, through the trunk, past a wrist's range, on a thing that gets further ahead of it than the joints, so pushed, keep up with, or taken out of the scene, is an error once, and is let go; a thing that hangs from the limb's own bones is an error at the statement.

`body.hand.left.value` is where the wrist is. Unsaid, the hand lies in line with the forearm. Sent with a `palm` or `pointing`, `{ x, y, z }` ways counted as its place is, in the scene, from him as he faces for a place on him, or in the frame of the object, it arrives with the palm facing and the fingers, or what it carries, pointing so; arriving moving, it is turned so at that moment only, and at rest stays so while it stays there: said both, the palm is as said and the hand points as near as that leaves it, and said only a palm, as near along the forearm. Where the wrist cannot turn the hand so, or the arm would be in the trunk, the elbow swings out or in about the line from the shoulder to the hand, as little as does and up to 1.2 rad. The forearm twists up to 1.6 rad either way and the wrist bends 1.3 rad forward and back, 0.35 to the side of the thumb and 0.6 to the other, as strong as a forearm; what that does not reach, a way that leads nowhere, or a palm to face the way the hand points, is an error at the statement. Sent `with` a fingertip, `'thumb'`, `'index'`, `'middle'`, `'ring'` or `'little'`, or the `'palm'`, its middle, the place and the velocity are that part's, as the fingers are then, whenever their pose is stated; `value` is still the wrist. A hand that arrives moving has its wrist drawn back with the arm, within its range, turning on with the motion as it arrives, and going on in the swing-out.

A place in the hand is `{ position, quaternion }`: a thing as it sits in the frame of the rig's hand bone, which is what `grip` of [`@drawcall/hand`](../hand) gives with the pose that holds it. Sent `with` one, the place and the velocity are the thing's origin, and `pointing` its z: a thing carried, hung on the hand bone there, goes where it is sent. Sent to one `on` a thing, the hand holds that thing as it says, and keeps it as the thing moves; a `palm`, `pointing` or `with` said with it is an error, as it says how the hand is turned already. With `gives: thing` as well, the thing gives way to the hands that hold it, the least that has each reach it and keeps it out of him, and `rests: point` has that point of it rest in the pocket of the hand's shoulder, the thing sliding there turned as it was set. `body.look(place, { over: point, on: thing })` has him look down such a thing, that point of it brought onto the line from his eye to the place and his head down onto it; `body.eye(side)` is where that eye is in the scene.

The fingers start relaxed: lightly curled, each a little more than the one before it from the index to the little finger, the thumb resting against the side of the index. A pose is how they are held, finger by finger: `{ thumb, index, middle, ring, little }`, each `{ spread, bend: [knuckle, middle, last] }` in rad, a spread turning a finger toward the thumb's side and a bend folding a joint toward the palm; the thumb's first bend lifts it in front of the palm. The two hands in one pose mirror each other, and `@drawcall/hand` makes such poses, named or gripping a mesh. `hand.shape(pose)` has them take it in the time fingers take, a quarter of a second for a man from flat to a fist, or `by` a moment, setting off as late as they can for it, and hold it until they are given another, whatever the hand does: `free()` and `stand()` leave it. `hand.shape()` relaxes them. A pose that leaves out a finger, or turns a joint further than a person's does, is an error at the statement. A body holds nothing itself: a thing in the hand is hung on the rig's hand bone where its grip has it sit, made of the rig at rest, before the body is, and stays there as the fingers open to let it go. A walk that ends on a `plant` foot has its strides come out even onto it, and he stays on it, the other foot where it was, until he is sent on, a foot is let `free` or he is told to `stand`; a foot or a hand that arrives moving swings out, comes round, and is the body's again. What the story has otherwise sent stays the story's until it is let `free`; what it is sent to next it goes on to when that has to set off, holding the first until then. What cannot be done is an error at the statement where that can be told then, and otherwise when it shows, thrown once by `update` after the step it showed in, his clock going on: a part pushed more than twice as hard as it is strong, a place out of reach, a joint that does not turn that far, a hand or a foot that was to stay somewhere and is taken out of reach, or a way along the ground his legs do not keep up with, no foot under him for longer than he is in the air between the strides of a run: he has fallen.

He walks at up to 0.85 of his pace and runs at up to 3.4, which for a man is 2.5 m/s and 10; the hips go from 0.32 of `size.stand` up to it, and no lower than 0.74 of it sent along at a walk. Low, they ride back behind his feet, so a squat ends with them short of the place they were sent along z. After a run he is still going: `walk` somewhere far enough for him to come to rest, or `stand()` first.

Standing, he turns on the spot to within 0.6 rad of where he is to go, 2.3 rad/s for a man: head and trunk lead, the pelvis follows, and his feet step round after it, each put on ahead of the turn, four steps to a half turn; the rest he makes as he sets off. Going, the way he goes turns as hard as a leg pushes, 6 m/s² across, and over a foot on the ground only as far as keeps it clear of his other leg, so that he comes round in the half of each stride that opens his stance, a quarter turn in two strides at a walk; and he goes no faster than has him round by the time he is there. A foot on the ground stays as it stands while he turns over it. A place within a stride of him, 0.85 of his leg, he steps to without turning, backwards or sideways, and faces as he did; one further off behind him he turns round to. A walk ends at rest: to go on through a place, round a corner, `run` to it at the speed he walks, which is a walk, and over as he comes by.

With a way to `face`, he walks any way. Up to an eighth of a turn off it his pelvis turns to the way he goes and his trunk keeps the facing: an ordinary walk, at up to 0.85 of his pace. Square to it he sidesteps: the foot on that side steps out half a leg's length, to a stance a shoulder's width and a half, the other closes to the width of his hips, the hips a little lower, about two steps a second, at up to 0.5 of his pace (1.45 m/s). Facing away he backs, at up to 0.5 of his pace. Between, the most he goes is between those, by how much of his going is along the way his pelvis faces, and he slows to it before he turns further from the way he goes. A way to face is for a walk: stated with a `run`, or while he runs, it is an error. `stand()` lets the way he faces go, and he stays facing as he does. A jump goes the way he is going as his legs begin to push: a top more than a step to the side of that is an error.

Led, `body.go(velocity)` each frame, he walks or runs by the speed asked and comes round as above, to within a turn made in four lengths of his leg; from a stand he first turns on the spot. Led faster than he walks he faces the way he goes whatever way to face is stated, and takes that up again as he is led slower, slowing to what it lets him go. He is not brought to a stand for want of a story, and stories played meanwhile, a hand, a kick, a jump, are planned on the way he is going when they are stated: led elsewhere before their moment, they are an error as any plan the body does not keep. A body does not know what is in his way: whoever leads him asks for what the world leaves him, and tells where it has stopped him. `hips.at` is for that, a correction of less than a stride: his feet stay where they stand and he steps after his hips. Put further off, he is not walked there but is there, feet and all, at rest if he was. A game that moves a character through its world, a capsule that walks, falls and jumps, leads him by it: `go` with how it goes, and in the air how fast it rises or falls too, and `hips.at` with where it is and how high the ground under it is.

```ts
body.go(velocity) // how he is to be going, as the walls let him
body.update(delta)
const stopped = pushedOut(body.hips.value) // the game's own: where its walls let him be
if (stopped) body.hips.at(stopped)
```

Time goes on in steps of a sixtieth of a beat, 1/200 s for a man, however `update` is called, and a moment, one that is waited for or one a hand or a foot is given, is the first step at or after it.

## Examples

The examples are in `../../examples`. `football` is a run-up and a shot at a ball, `javelin` a run-up and a javelin throw, `bowling` a bowling delivery, `basketball` a jump shot, `shelf` a box picked up and put on a shelf, `boxing` a jab and a cross at a bag, `fencing` an advance, a lunge and a recovery, `tennis` a forehand, `volleyball` a spike, `hurdles` a sprint over two hurdles, `button` a button pressed with a fingertip, `shooter` a soldier aimed by the mouse who shoots, reloads, throws a grenade and presses a button, a model rigged as it loads with `@drawcall/rig`, his arms a quarter shorter than the mannequin's, `rifleman` a soldier led by the keyboard who reloads and throws a grenade, `patrol` a square walked round without a stop, a sidestep along its edge and a turn on the spot, and `led` a man led by the keyboard among boxes, with no story at all. Each is its own small Vite app that brings its prop and its story, and those with something in the hand take its grip and pose from `@drawcall/hand`, which `carry.ts` hangs on the hand bone; the character the others share is `mannequin.ts`, a man of rods skinned to the skeleton in `person.ts`, on whose bare bones the tests stand a body, and on those of `other()` there, a made-up man with short arms, bent wrists and the thumb along the index.

```sh
pnpm install
pnpm -F football dev   # or: pnpm -F javelin dev
pnpm test              # three of the examples, the rig, sizes, the ground, the hands, going any way and the limits, headless
pnpm check             # types
```
