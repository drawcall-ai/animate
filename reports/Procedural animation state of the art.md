# Classical control outranks learning for animate-2d

As of November 2026, research in character animation is dominated by reinforcement-learned motion trackers distilled into generative controllers, but none of that work transfers to a dependency-free 2D browser library: it needs mocap, GPU-parallel simulators and 10^9 to 10^10 samples, and no current research targets planar stick figures at all. The techniques that would most improve animate-2d are older and smaller. Four changes address almost every listed weakness: (1) replace explicit PD force pairs and hard 8-iteration rods with implicit joint drives and XPBD compliance, which removes the 1/1440 s lock-in; (2) add a per-particle ground inequality with friction and a depenetration cap, which stops the figure sinking; (3) replace scripted foot placement with capture-point feedback and give every wait a timeout, a failure state and a typed result, which ends the hangs; (4) generalise the 1-D bisection into a scan-then-refine search over a residual vector that returns an achievable range and a signed margin instead of silently returning the cap. The library's "rehearse on a clone" primitive is already the core of sampling-based model-predictive control, the one model-based method that had a visible revival in robotics in 2024 to 2026. Its generator stories sit in a thirty-year lineage of phase state machines; what that lineage adds is contracts and failure paths. The main uncertainty is quantitative: no source measures how large a step an XPBD point-mass biped tolerates or how many rollouts per frame single-threaded JavaScript affords, so those numbers need measurement in the library itself.

## Research went to learned trackers that a small library cannot use

The dominant recipe since 2023 is to train a large motion-tracking policy with reinforcement learning on thousands of mocap clips, then distil it into a controller that text, keyframes or a planner can drive. The scale is the point. PHC tracks 98.9% of AMASS training sequences after about a week on one A100 and roughly 10 billion samples ([PHC](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.06456)); MaskedMimic reframes control as inpainting from any subset of keyframes, text, objects and paths ([MaskedMimic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14393)); NVIDIA's SONIC, the largest published instance, uses **42M parameters, 100M+ frames and up to 21k GPU-hours** ([SONIC](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07820v3)). Diffusion controllers such as UniPhys and MIND are behaviour-cloned from those trackers and run at about 10 to 18 FPS ([UniPhys](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12540), [MIND](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26006)).

Two facts from this literature matter for animate-2d. First, learned controllers do not hit outcome targets exactly either. PhysicsFC, the 2025 football controller, takes a target ball velocity as its goal input and scores the ball's velocity in a 1/3 s window after contact; over 1,000 random targets it reaches **4.79° mean direction error and 4.51 m/s mean speed error**, after 30 hours of training for the kick alone on top of a 30-day low-level policy ([PhysicsFC](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21216)). A rehearsed, searched torque recipe that lands within a stated tolerance is competitive with that. Second, the planar niche is empty. The only comparable artefact found is an old browser 2D ragdoll trained with DDPG that needed 8 to 44 GPU-hours to walk "ish" ([rl_2d_walker.js](https://www.github.com/wassname/rl_2d_walker.js)); two targeted searches found no 2025 to 2026 work on learned control of 2D stick figures. Inference is cheap (a 164K-parameter recurrent policy runs a 29-DoF humanoid in browser WASM, per a community model card ([nanoG1](https://huggingface.co/kingJulio/nanoG1))), but the browser demos work because training and deployment share one physics engine. A bespoke TypeScript simulator has no GPU training twin, so a learned policy would have to be trained inside the library's own engine.

What transfers is structure: a robust low-level balance and tracking layer under a high-level intent layer, recovery from falls as its own primitive (PHC's recovers in 98.8% of cases), testing each skill from the end states of its predecessor (PhysicsFC), and the finding that outcome reward without a motion prior fails. In SMPLOlympics, plain PPO scored 0% on the penalty kick ([SMPLOlympics](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00187)). In a procedural system the hand-authored recipe plays the role of the prior.

Game-industry practice confirms the same division. Motion matching is the mainstream AAA locomotion method and needs a mocap database. Every shipped physically driven character that could be sourced keeps upright with explicit non-physical help: Landfall's TABS pushes up on torso and head through an authored height-to-force curve and moves a foot back when both feet are ahead of the centre of mass ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-landfall-games-finds-the-fun-in-physics-engines)), and ARC Raiders, the first clearly documented shipped learned physics locomotion, still uses "magic forces" that catch a falling character, after five to ten people worked on it for years and it was nearly cut several times ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/inside-the-magic-of-machine-learning-that-powers-enemy-ai-in-arc-raiders)). The library's hip ground-force servo is therefore standard practice, not a compromise unique to hand-written control. Exanima's developer states the limit plainly: generating an action procedurally "under any condition is simply not possible" ([Bare Mettle forums](https://www.baremettle.com/forums/index.php?threads/about-the-parry-mechanic.5293/)).

## Training-free sampling control revived the model-based side

In robotics, model-based control came back through methods that need no training. MuJoCo MPC's predictive sampling perturbs spline control parameters, rolls out N candidates from the current state, keeps the best and warm-starts the next plan; its authors introduced it as deliberately crude and found it "surprisingly competitive" with iLQG, with total planning time of **1 to 20 ms per update on a single CPU** ([Howell et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.00541)). Whole-body MPPI then ran on a real quadruped with **only 30 rollouts** over a 0.4 s horizon ([arXiv 2409.10469](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.10469)), and DIAL-MPC claims parity with RL on some agile tasks plus instant redeployment when the model changes ([arXiv 2409.15610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15610)). The 2026 frontier is hybrid: a cheap prior proposes, sampling refines against an explicit cost ([RGB, arXiv 2606.25123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25123)).

This is the frame in which to read animate-2d's "drive". Rehearsing a torque recipe on a cloned state is single shooting; bisecting one power parameter is a 1-D root finder on top of it. The literature's caveat applies directly: with local methods, "infeasible" cannot be distinguished from "stuck" ([Tedrake, trajectory optimization](https://underactuated.mit.edu/trajopt.html)), so the honest contract returns the best result found with a status rather than a proof. Gradient methods (collocation, iLQR, contact-invariant optimisation) need derivatives through contact that a PBD simulator does not expose, and they are poor value here compared with sampling.

For balance, every classical stepping rule reduces to placing the swing foot at a linear function of centre-of-mass velocity. SIMBICON's swing-hip feedback θd = θd0 + cd·d + cv·v withstood 0.1 s pushes of 600 N on a walking biped, with documented failure modes when the velocity gain is too low or too high ([SIMBICON](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)); Coros et al. used an inverted-pendulum foot target recomputed every timestep plus Jacobian-transpose gravity compensation and needed no per-character tuning ([GBWC](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)). A 2022 analysis gives closed-form bounds for the rule x_foot = a + b·v: with Tc = sqrt(h/g), the **capture-point gain b = Tc is the only constant gain stable for every step period**, the dead-beat gain is Tc·coth(T/Tc), and the stable interval widens as steps get faster ([Ye et al., arXiv 2211.06223](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.06223)). Both SIMBICON and GBWC ran at one fixed small step (0.1 ms and 0.5 ms) with explicit PD, the same class of timestep brittleness animate-2d shows.

## The position-based lineage fixed each of animate-2d's simulator defects

The library is 2007-style PBD: hard rods, eight iterations, velocities from positions. Its successors address its defects one by one. XPBD exists to solve "the long-standing problem of iteration count and time step dependent constraint stiffness" by adding a compliance term ([XPBD](https://mmacklin.com/xpbd.pdf)). Small Steps shows that n substeps of one iteration beat one step of n iterations, because position error from external forces scales with Δt²: a hanging chain's maximum error fell from 322.1 m with 100 iterations to 3.2 m with 100 substeps ([Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf)). Müller's 2020 rigid-body paper then expresses actuation as a constraint, not a force: a hinge is driven toward a target angle with a compliance that sets its stiffness, the applied torque is read back from the multiplier, joint damping is a velocity pass clamped by min(μ·h, 1) so it is unconditionally stable, and "compliance, forces and elongation are in the correct relation independent of the substep" ([Müller et al. 2020](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf)). Engines agree: Box2D v3's Soft Step solver combines substepping with soft constraints parameterised by frequency and damping ratio ([Solver2D](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/02/solver2d/)), and Rapier's joint motors are spring-like position drives solved inside the solver ([Rapier joints](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/joints)).

The controls literature reaches the same conclusion from the other side. Explicit PD couples high gain to a small timestep; Stable PD evaluates the PD law at the next step, which in one degree of freedom is q̈ = −[kp(q + Δt·q̇ − q̄) + kd·q̇] / (m + kd·Δt), stable for kd ≥ kp·Δt. At Δt = 1/60 s it tracked with kp = 30,000 where conventional PD crashed the simulation, and unlike a fully implicit integrator it does not over-damp timing-critical motion ([Tan, Liu, Turk 2011](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf)). That caveat matters for kicks and throws.

The newest solver family, Augmented Vertex Block Descent (SIGGRAPH 2025), holds hard joints and stacking at 1/60 s with four or five iterations and has an MIT 2D reference implementation ([AVBD paper](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/Augmented_VBD-SIGGRAPH25.pdf), [avbd-demo2d](https://github.com/savant117/avbd-demo2d)). It is a rewrite to rigid bodies with per-body Hessian solves, and its paper does not mention motors, so XPBD with substeps is the incremental path for a point-mass figure.

Contact follows the same pattern. Ground contact in this family is an inequality on every body, with static friction applied in the position pass only while the tangential multiplier stays under μ times the normal one, dynamic friction as a clamped velocity update, and a cap on depenetration speed, because deriving velocity from positions turns penetration d₀ into a separating velocity d₀/Δt ([Müller et al. 2020](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf), [Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf)). At 1/1440 s, a 1 mm penetration becomes 1.44 m/s of pop, a plausible source of foot jitter.

Adopting an engine is not justified by this evidence. Rapier offers cross-platform determinism and whole-world snapshots ([Rapier determinism](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/determinism)), and Box2D 3.2 lists snapshots as experimental with efficient rollback as future work ([Box2D 3.2](https://box2d.org/posts/2026/10/box2d-3.2/)); neither is shown to support thousands of clone-and-restore cycles, which is exactly what the drive needs and what a typed-array copy of a dozen particles gives for free. No maintained browser-native 2D active-ragdoll reference was found, so the bespoke simulator fills a gap rather than duplicating a library.

## Eight concrete changes, ordered by leverage

The table maps each known weakness to the technique the research supports. The paragraphs after it give the reasoning and the order of work. Where a recommendation rests on the note-takers' engineering inference and not a published result, the text says so.

| Weakness | Recommended change | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Correct only at 1/1440 s | XPBD compliance on rods; joint drives as implicit PD (compliant three-particle angle constraint, or Stable PD with an effective inertia); damping as rates; substeps with one iteration | XPBD, Small Steps, Müller 2020, Stable PD |
| Figure sinks through floor | One ground inequality per particle with radius, Coulomb friction, depenetration-speed cap | Müller 2020, Small Steps |
| Balance hangs forever | Capture-point foot target with b = Tc; reach test; explicit fall and get-up states; every wait raced against a timeout | LFPC bounds, GBWC, Faloutsos 2001, SIMBICON |
| 1-D bisection, silent cap, non-monotonic response | Coarse scan of the parameter box, then local refinement on a residual vector; return status, achievable range and signed margin | Predictive sampling, Tedrake, STL robustness |
| About fifty hand-tuned constants | Frequency and damping ratio for drives, half-lives for smoothing, gains derived from h, g and mass; offline CMA-ES for the remainder | Unreal Physics Control, Holden, GBWC, Wang et al. 2009 |
| Recipes touch internal flags | Typed intents with results, named sync points, combinators; assists exposed as bounded world-space controls | Faloutsos, BML, Motion Canvas, StateTree |
| Torques several times human | Gravity compensation, torque limits by clamping the drive multiplier, proximal-to-distal sequencing checked against biomechanics | GBWC, SIMBICON, Kellis and Katis 2007 |
| No failure paths | Typed outcome `succeeded / failed / interrupted` plus a per-tick expected-performance guard | Faloutsos, StateTree, behaviour trees |

### Fix the integrator first, because it gates everything else

The timestep lock-in has two sources named in the brief, and each has a published cure. Rod stiffness that depends on iteration count is what XPBD compliance removes. Explicit PD force pairs are conditionally stable in gain times step, which is what implicit drives remove. For a point-mass figure with no body orientation, the hinge target becomes an angle constraint over three particles solved with the XPBD multiplier update. That mapping is the researchers' inference, since no primary source derives implicit PD inside a PBD solver, so it needs a test against the Stable PD form as a fallback. Damping should become a rate applied as exp(−rate·dt) or Müller's clamped velocity pass, not a per-step multiplier. With those in place the outer loop is a fixed-step accumulator with render interpolation ([Fix Your Timestep](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/)), and substep count becomes a budget choice.

The payoff is larger than correctness. Eight iterations at 1/1440 s is 11,520 constraint passes per simulated second. The researchers estimate that moving to 240 to 480 Hz with one iteration cuts cost per simulated second by roughly 24 to 48 times, the difference between a few and a few hundred half-second rehearsals per frame. That figure is an estimate, not a measurement, and the first task after the change is to measure rollouts per frame and the largest stable step. Determinism must be preserved throughout, because rehearsal only works if the clone reproduces the real run.

### Give every particle a floor and read the ground force from it

Each particle gets a radius and one inequality against the ground: project up when below it, accumulate the normal multiplier, apply friction against μ times that multiplier, and cap depenetration speed. With a dozen particles the cost is negligible next to the rod solves. The side benefit is that the accumulated multiplier divided by h² is the ground reaction force, which the balance layer needs and which the current feet-only clamp cannot supply. The mapping from the rigid-body papers to point masses is again inference, but it is a direct specialisation.

### Replace scripted placement with feedback, and let balance fail

The smallest change with the largest robustness payoff is to add the velocity term to the foot target: x_foot = x_com + b·v_com minus an offset for desired speed, with b = sqrt(h/g), about 0.32 s for a 1 m hip height, recomputed during the swing as GBWC does. This turns magic numbers into functions of h and g and yields a feasibility test: if the required step exceeds leg reach, one step cannot recover, and the controller should step again or fall on purpose. The Last Guardian's rules are a ready checklist for the scripted part: choose the landing point at lift-off, reject bad candidates, recheck mid-swing, pin on contact ([Tanaka, CEDEC 2017](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1cYDabTQGYe09w3PyUU4XLrDn2fHy9X5eJIFUjVDt89U/htmlpresent)).

Shipped systems treat falling as a state transition, not something the balancer must always prevent. PuppetMaster models loss and recovery of balance as explicit behaviours with a "regain balance" event ([RootMotion](https://rootmotion.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/77000484277-patches)), and control graphs ship dedicated stand-up skills ([Liu et al. 2016](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2016-TOG-controlGraphs/2016-TOG-controlGraphs.pdf)). For animate-2d this means settle gets a timeout and a failure result, a fall releases control to passive physics, and get-up is its own story.

### Turn the drive into a search that reports what is reachable

The upgrade path runs in three steps of rising cost. First, define a residual vector (speed error, angle error, release-height error) and a tolerance; a javelin release has at least speed and angle, which one parameter cannot hit except by luck. Second, scan the parameter box coarsely before refining. The scan is what detects non-monotonic response, which is expected physically because more power disturbs balance and contact timing, and it produces the achievable range that converts the silent cap into "27 m/s is outside what this figure can do; best is 24 m/s". Third, refine the best basin with a few Gauss-Newton steps on finite differences, or with predictive-sampling or CEM-style resampling when the response is discontinuous. Keeping several diverse candidates per segment instead of the single best is SAMCON's remedy for greedy dead ends ([SAMCON](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-sampControl/2010-TOG-samcon.pdf)).

The result type should follow the formal-methods literature, the only place with a principled answer to infeasibility: signal temporal logic assigns a signed robustness degree to a specification ([Gu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15993)), and relaxation work reports the smallest loosening that would have succeeded ([Buyukkocak and Aksaray](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08384v2)). A drive that returns `converged`, `target outside range` or `budget exhausted`, with the margin, serves both human and LLM authors better than a boolean. PhysicsFC's errors of about 5° and 4.5 m/s set a realistic bar for tolerance.

### Shrink the constants and the torques together

Engine tooling and practitioner writing converge on the same units. Unreal's Physics Control parameterises drives by strength and damping ratio, with 1 meaning critical damping, plus an extra-damping term that works at zero strength ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/PhysicsControl/FPhysicsControlData)); Holden's exact springs use half-life and are stable for any dt ([Spring-It-On](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call)). Exact springs suit target and parameter smoothing; they cannot replace joint drives inside the coupled solve. Expressing each drive as frequency and damping ratio against an effective inertia, and each foot gain through Tc, should collapse many of the fifty constants into a few physical ones; that consolidation is inference, not a measured result. For what remains, CMA-ES run headless on the library's own simulator has precedent in the optimisation of SIMBICON parameters ([Wang, Fleet, Hertzmann 2009](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~jmwang/optwalk)) and avoids any sim-to-sim gap.

Torque magnitude has three sourced levers. Gravity compensation is "key to achieving successful motions without resorting to excessively high PD gains" (GBWC). Torque limits act as a plausibility device: SIMBICON clamps at 1000 N·m, walks at 370 and survives down to 90. And biomechanics gives targets: in maximal instep kicks peak hip-flexion moments are roughly **190 to 310 N·m and knee-extension moments 70 to 160 N·m**, the thigh peaks near 516 to 573 deg/s before the shank reaches about 1,900 deg/s, and the knee extensor moment falls to zero or reverses just before impact while interaction moments finish the acceleration ([Kellis and Katis 2007](https://www.jssm.org/volume06/iss2/cap/jssm-06-154.pdf), [Lees et al. 2010](https://numerik.mi.fu-berlin.de/wiki/WS_2020/CoMaI_Dokumente/FTVS-2332-version1-the_biomechanics_of_kicking_in_soccer_a_review.pdf)). A drive that scales distal torque until the ball reaches 27 m/s gets the number with the wrong mechanics. Sequencing proximal joints first, bracing the plant leg and clamping the drive multiplier should bring torques toward human values; whether it fully closes a several-fold gap in a 2D point-mass model is untested. The targets themselves are realistic: 27 m/s sits within experienced players' ball speeds, and 28.1 to 29.7 m/s is the release speed of 84 to 90 m javelin throws ([Campos et al.](https://www.uv.es/jcampos/Documentos/NEA_IAAF_MenJavelin.pdf)), so the javelin target is elite-level.

### Give stories contracts so recipes stop touching flags

A generator story is a phase state machine whose program counter is the state, the same shape as SIMBICON and Hodgins-era controllers. Faloutsos et al. supply what a bare generator lacks: pre-conditions, post-conditions and an "expected performance" predicate that detects failure "at any point during its operation" so another controller can take over ([Faloutsos et al. 2001](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2001-siggraph-composable.pdf)). Unreal's StateTree routes Succeeded and Failed completions separately ([Epic StateTree](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/overview-of-state-tree-in-unreal-engine)). SIMBICON pairs contact exits with elapsed-time exits and skips a state whose exit event has already occurred, a real edge case for a wait on contact.

In practice: every intent and story returns a typed result; a guard runs every simulation tick, not only at yields; every wait is `any(event, timeout)` using Motion Canvas's established combinator names ([Motion Canvas](https://motioncanvas.io/docs/flow)); long intents expose BML-style named sync points such as ready, stroke and relax ([BML 1.0](https://projects.cs.ru.is/projects/behavior-markup-language/wiki)) so other intents align to them without reading flags; and "plant" becomes a first-class intent with a braking outcome, since the block leg does the same job in kick and javelin. Non-physical assists should be exposed as explicit, bounded, fadeable world-space controls, separate from parent-space muscles, and split into independent handlers as Landfall's third rewrite did. Body-relative parameters and defaults for everything unspecified keep sparse scripts valid.

## Conclusion

The research frontier and the right engineering for this library point in different directions, and that is not a sign the library is behind. Learned controllers moved to a scale where their artefacts are unusable here, while the methods that fit (implicit drives, compliant constraints, capture-point stepping, rehearsal-based search, contracts on skills) are considered solved for research purposes and are therefore well documented and cheap to adopt. The dependency chain decides the order: the integrator change makes rollouts cheap, cheap rollouts make multi-parameter search and eventually receding-horizon balance affordable, and typed results from both are what let stories fail cleanly.

Two open points deserve honesty. Nothing in the literature guarantees that a hand-written balancer generalises outside tuned scenarios; practitioners who shipped these systems say it does not, which is why bounded assists and a deliberate fall are part of the design, not a fallback. And no source was found in which an LLM or agent authors procedural physics-controller code against an intent API, or in which a small planar policy is cloned from a hand-written controller. Both are open territory where animate-2d, once its simulator is step-independent and its stories report outcomes, would be producing evidence instead of consuming it.
