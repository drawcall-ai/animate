# 2D simulation methods, contact handling, and browser / differentiable tooling (state as of November 2026)

Context for the writer: the target is a small dependency-free TypeScript library simulating a 2D stick figure as point masses joined by rigid rods (PBD-style projection, 8 iterations, velocities derived from positions), joint torques as equal-and-opposite force pairs, only the feet clamped to the ground, stable only at a fixed 1/1440 s step.

Verification legend: findings marked **[read]** were read in the primary document during this research session (paper PDF text or official page). Findings marked **[search]** come from search-result summaries of the linked page and were not opened in full. Anything I could only recall from background knowledge is in *Gaps*, not in *Cited Findings*.

## 1. Position-based dynamics lineage: what each method fixes, and how torques / angular constraints / motors are expressed

### Takeaway
The lineage is a sequence of fixes to exactly the defects the author's simulator has: XPBD (2016) removes iteration- and timestep-dependent stiffness by adding compliance; Small Steps (2019) shows that n substeps of 1 iteration beat 1 step of n iterations; Müller 2020 adds joint limits, target-angle motors, joint damping, static/dynamic Coulomb friction and restitution on top; AVBD (SIGGRAPH 2025) gets hard constraints and high stiffness ratios stable at 1/60 s with about 4-5 iterations and no substepping. None of these papers express actuation as raw force pairs; they express it as a compliant target-angle constraint (an implicit PD spring) plus a velocity-level damping pass, which is the main route to large-step stability.

### Cited Findings

**PBD (Müller et al. 2007) as characterised by its successors**
- **[read]** In PBD the stiffness coefficient k in [0,1] is applied as a simple scale on the correction, so "the effective constraint stiffness is now dependent on both the time step and the number of constraint projections performed"; constraints become arbitrarily stiff as iteration count increases or the time step decreases, and the relationship is non-linear so stiffness cannot be rescaled as a simple function of iteration count. — [Macklin, Müller, Chentanez, "XPBD: Position-Based Simulation of Compliant Constrained Dynamics", MIG 2016](https://mmacklin.com/xpbd.pdf)
- **[read]** Non-linear projected Gauss-Seidel as used in PBD handles each constraint with current gradients (minimising overshoot), is robust for over-constrained systems, and handles unilateral (inequality) constraints trivially by projecting only violated ones; its weakness is slow error propagation on stiff problems and numerical dissipation. — [Macklin et al., "Small Steps in Physics Simulation", SCA 2019](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf)

**XPBD (Macklin, Müller, Chentanez 2016)**
- **[read]** Stated goal: "We address the long-standing problem of iteration count and time step dependent constraint stiffness in position-based dynamics", via a new constraint formulation with a compliance matrix (inverse stiffness) derived from an elastic potential, with the time step folded into the compliance. — [XPBD paper](https://mmacklin.com/xpbd.pdf)
- **[read]** Per-constraint update (as restated in Small Steps): Δx = M⁻¹ ∇Cᵢᵀ Δλᵢ with Δλᵢ = (−Cᵢ(x) − α̃ᵢ λᵢ) / (∇Cᵢ M⁻¹ ∇Cᵢᵀ + α̃ᵢ); in the rigid-body paper α̃ = α/h² where α is the compliance and h the substep. α = 0 gives an infinitely stiff constraint. — [Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf); [Müller et al. 2020](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf)
- **[read]** The first iteration of XPBD is equivalent to the first step of a Newton solver on the backward-Euler equations, so a single-iteration scheme inherits implicit-method stability while costing about as much as explicit integration. — [Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf)
- **[read]** The Lagrange multiplier gives a usable force estimate (f = λ/h²); in a hanging-chain test the estimate at the top constraint converged to the true 190 N even with one iteration per substep. — [Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf)

**Small Steps in Physics Simulation (Macklin et al., SCA 2019)**
- **[read]** Central claim: "performing a single large time step with n constraint solver iterations is less effective than computing n smaller time steps, each with a single constraint solver iteration"; "for PGS the optimum lies at the far end ... taking as many small steps as the time budget allows while performing a single iteration in each step". — [Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf)
- **[read]** Reason: the effect of external forces on positions is proportional to Δt², so "halving the time step will result in a quarter of the position error". Hanging chain with 1:100000 mass ratio: max error 322.1 m with 100 iterations vs 3.2 m with 100 substeps (two orders of magnitude). — [Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf)
- **[read]** Algorithm 1: collision detection once per frame; then for each substep: predict x̃ = xⁿ + Δtₛ vⁿ + Δtₛ² M⁻¹ f_ext; one pass over all constraints; v = (xⁿ⁺¹ − xⁿ)/Δtₛ. — [Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf)
- **[read]** Substepping reduces numerical damping so much that explicit damping must be added back; the paper uses the XPBD damping term (γᵢ = α̃ᵢ β̃ᵢ / Δtₛ) inside the multiplier update. — [Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf)
- **[read]** Cost is similar per pass: cloth example 1.8 ms for 40 XPBD iterations vs 2.4 ms for 40 substeps; jointed rigid chain with 1:1000 mass ratio: 100 iterations 4 ms with visible joint separation, 500 iterations 17 ms still stretching, 100 substeps x 1 iteration 6 ms "stiff and stable". — [Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf)
- **[read]** Contact: inelastic inequality constraint C = nᵀ(a − b) ≥ 0; because a small step turns initial penetration d₀ into a separating velocity d₀/Δtₛ ("artificial elastic popping"), the constraint is relaxed to nᵀ(a−b) + max(d₀ − v_max Δtₛ, 0) ≥ 0, capping depenetration speed. Friction: frictional attachment constraint with the multiplier clamped, Δλ_f ← min(μ Δλ_n, Δλ_f). — [Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf)
- **[read]** Even at infinite stiffness (zero compliance) the method stays stable and "will behave as stiff as possible given the total number of substeps", while semi-implicit Euler diverges. — [Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf)

**Detailed Rigid Body Simulation with XPBD (Müller, Macklin, Chentanez, Jeschke, Kim; CGF 39(8), SCA 2020)**
- **[read]** Loop: h = Δt/numSubsteps; per substep integrate, run numPosIters position passes, derive velocities from positions, then a velocity-level pass. "It is significantly more effective to take substeps than solver iterations so numPosIters is typically set to 1"; "the best choice is to replace all solver iterations by substeps". — [Müller et al. 2020](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf)
- **[read]** Motivation: velocity-level or implicit solvers linearise and freeze constraint directions across iterations; this method always uses the most recent constraint directions, which matters for fast curved motion. — [Eurographics DL record](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14105)
- **[read]** Actuation is a constraint, not a force: "To drive a hinge joint towards a specified target angle α we rotate ..."; "The corresponding compliance α controls the stiffness of the constraint. With a target angle constraint we can create a velocity driven motor by updating the target angle via α ← α + h v at every substep, where v is the target velocity of the motor and the corresponding compliance its strength." A zero-compliance target angle "applies the correct torque to hold the bar straight", and the torque can be read back from the multiplier. — [Müller et al. 2020](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf)
- **[read]** A prescribed force f on a positional constraint is obtained by choosing the compliance α = (|Δr| − d_target)/f, i.e. force control is expressible within the same projection. — [Müller et al. 2020](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf)
- **[read]** Joint limits: a generic LimitAngle(n, n1, n2, α, β) routine clamps the angle between two body axes and applies an angular correction; "All limits can be made soft by using α > 0". — [Müller et al. 2020](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf)
- **[read]** Joint damping lives in the velocity pass: Δv ← (v₂ − v₁) min(μ_lin h, 1), Δω ← (ω₂ − ω₁) min(μ_ang h, 1); the min(…, 1) clamp makes it unconditionally stable. — [Müller et al. 2020](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf)
- **[read]** "Compliance, forces and elongation are in the correct relation independent of the substep and" iteration count (demonstrated with a 1 g box above a 1 kg box; 20 substeps leaves only a small error). — [Müller et al. 2020](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf)
- **[read]** Mass ratio of 1:760 (servo arm vs wheels) handled by substepping in the RC-car demo. — [Müller et al. 2020](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf)
- **[read]** Matthias Müller's "Ten Minute Physics" has 25 tutorials, most as single-file HTML/JavaScript browser demos: #06 triple pendulum, #09 "Getting ready to simulate the world with XPBD", #22 "How to write a basic rigid body simulator using position based dynamics", #25 "Joint simulation made simple" (XPBD joints). — [Ten Minute Physics](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/tenMinutePhysics/index.html)

**Erin Catto's Solver2D comparison (Feb 2024): the engine-side verdict on XPBD**
- **[read]** Eight Gauss-Seidel solvers compared in a stripped-down Box2D v3 at 60 Hz, 4 primary + 2 secondary iterations: PGS (Baumgarte), PGS_NGS, PGS_NGS_Block, PGS_Soft, TGS_Sticky, TGS_Soft, TGS_NGS, XPBD. TGS_Soft (substepping + warm starting + soft constraints + relaxation) was chosen for Box2D v3 and renamed "Soft Step". — [Solver2D post](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/02/solver2d/); [Releasing Box2D 3.0](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/08/releasing-box2d-3.0)
- **[read]** Catto agrees smaller steps beat more iterations, and makes substepping cheap by storing contact points in local coordinates so separation is updated without re-running collision detection. — [Solver2D post](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/02/solver2d/)
- **[read]** XPBD problems he hit: deriving velocity from (position − position0) far from the origin loses significant digits, bodies stop moving and the simulation becomes unstable; fix is to solve on a delta-position and update the absolute position at the end of the step. He also "had trouble getting friction to work well with XPBD" (acknowledging possible implementation mistakes). — [Solver2D post](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/02/solver2d/)
- **[read]** Soft constraints are parameterised as a damped harmonic oscillator by frequency (hertz) and damping ratio ζ, from which mass-independent bias-rate / mass / impulse coefficients are computed for step h; hertz should be well below the step rate (30 Hz or less at a 60 Hz step). A "relax" pass re-solves with bias and softness off to remove energy the stabilisation added; "Relaxation is expensive, but it improves the simulation quality dramatically." — [Solver2D post](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/02/solver2d/)

**Vertex Block Descent (Chen, Macklin, Müller, Kim, Yuksel; SIGGRAPH 2024)**
- **[search]** VBD is a block coordinate descent solver for the variational form of implicit Euler using vertex-level Gauss-Seidel iterations (arXiv 2403.06321, last revised June 2024). — [arXiv 2403.06321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06321); [Anka Chen's page](https://ankachan.github.io/)
- **[read]** As characterised in the AVBD paper: VBD is a primal method (updates positions per vertex/body by a small local Newton step) that stays stable with few iterations and colours better than XPBD for parallelism, but "struggles to converge for problems involving high stiffness ratios" and has "no mechanism for enforcing explicit hard constraints"; VBD only sketches articulated bodies via joint constraints with an elastic potential. XPBD, as a dual method, "can result in significant error with high mass ratios". — [AVBD paper](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/Augmented_VBD-SIGGRAPH25.pdf)

**Augmented Vertex Block Descent (Giles, Diaz, Yuksel; ACM TOG 44(4), SIGGRAPH 2025, Article 90, August 2025)**
- **[read]** AVBD adds an augmented-Lagrangian dual update (λ) and a per-constraint penalty stiffness that ramps up each iteration after each primal VBD iteration, giving hard constraints with "infinite stiffness" without instability and better convergence at high stiffness ratios. It covers rigid-body contact with stacking and friction, articulated bodies with hard constraints, joints with limited degrees of freedom, and stiff/soft coupling. — [AVBD project page](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/); [AVBD paper](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/Augmented_VBD-SIGGRAPH25.pdf)
- **[read]** Fixed parameters for every example in the paper: timestep 1/60 s, β = 10 (stiffness ramp; "similar results for any β in [1, 1000]"), α = 0.95 (fraction of pre-existing constraint error corrected per step, so error is removed gradually rather than as an energy spike), γ = 0.99 (warm-start decay of stiffness and dual variables). — [AVBD paper](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/Augmented_VBD-SIGGRAPH25.pdf)
- **[read]** Inequality constraints (contacts, friction, joint limits) are handled by clamping λ between bounds, as in sequential impulses; friction cone via bounds ±μλₙ on the tangential multipliers, with static/dynamic coefficient switching based on whether the previous step was inside the cone. — [AVBD paper](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/Augmented_VBD-SIGGRAPH25.pdf)
- **[read]** Stiffness-ratio test: VBD with 5 iterations sags, 100 iterations still sags; substepping with 1 iteration helps but needs 30+ substeps; AVBD gets a good solution with 5 iterations and no substeps, and close with a single iteration thanks to warm starting. Chains of 20 articulated bodies hold with few iterations. — [AVBD paper](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/Augmented_VBD-SIGGRAPH25.pdf)
- **[read]** Scale: a pile of 110,000 blocks at 4 iterations takes 3.5 ms (9.8 ms with collision detection) on GPU. — [AVBD paper](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/Augmented_VBD-SIGGRAPH25.pdf)
- **[read]** Acknowledged limits: with a bounded iteration count, exact constraint satisfaction cannot be guaranteed; position-based methods "including our work, inject energy into the system to correct constraint error" (α < 1 mitigates this); implicit Euler (BDF1) dissipates energy and BDF2 is left as future work. The paper text does not mention motors. — [AVBD paper](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/Augmented_VBD-SIGGRAPH25.pdf)
- **[read]** Reference code: `avbd-demo2d` (MIT, C++ with CMake, native and Emscripten web build; 819 stars) and `avbd-demo3d`, plus online 2D and 3D demos linked from the project page; the README says it is meant as an easy-to-follow demonstration, not an optimised implementation. — [savant117/avbd-demo2d](https://github.com/savant117/avbd-demo2d); [AVBD project page](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/)
- **[search]** Community ports exist in TypeScript/WebGPU: `sbobyn/three-avbd` (TypeScript, Three.js, WebGPU) and "WebPhysics" (a WebGPU AVBD implementation by Jure Triglav). — [three-avbd](https://github.com/sbobyn/three-avbd); [WebPhysics showcase](https://webgpu.com/showcase/webphysics-webgpu-avbd-solver/)

**2025-2026 successors**
- **[read]** "Offset Geometric Contact" (ACM TOG 44(4), 2025): a contact model guaranteeing penetration-free simulation of codimensional objects at low cost. "Divide and Truncate: A Penetration and Inversion Free Framework for Coupled Multi-physics Systems" (SIGGRAPH 2026 Conference Papers): unified penetration- and inversion-free contact across rigid bodies, soft volumes, shells and rods, integrated into NVIDIA Newton's VBD solver. Note: the author's page lists OGC's venue as "SIGGRAPH Asia" while giving TOG 44(4), which is the SIGGRAPH (summer) issue number; treat the venue label as uncertain. — [Anka He Chen's publication list](https://ankachan.github.io/)
- **[search]** Anka Chen published a blog post "Implementing VBD Damping Properly" in March 2026. — [post](https://ankachan.github.io/posts/2026/03/implementing-vbd-properly/)
- **[read]** Newton 1.0 ships a VBD solver that can be explicitly coupled with MuJoCo Warp. — search summary of [Newton 1.0 GA announcement](https://github.gg22.workers.dev/newton-physics/newton/discussions/2176) (**[search]** for this item)

### Inferences
- The author's simulator is classic 2007-style PBD (hard rods, k = 1, 8 iterations, velocities from positions). Its behaviour is therefore a function of both dt and iteration count by construction; XPBD's compliance term is the minimal change that makes rod/spring stiffness a physical parameter independent of both.
- 8 iterations at 1/1440 s is 11,520 constraint passes per simulated second. Small Steps and Müller 2020 both say the same budget is better spent as 1 iteration x 8 substeps of a 1/180 s step, or equivalently that at 1/1440 s a single iteration would probably suffice. The current design pays for both small steps and many iterations.
- The stability problem at larger dt is most likely the explicit torque path rather than the rods: force pairs computed from a PD law are integrated explicitly (semi-implicit Euler inside the PBD predict step), which is conditionally stable in gain x dt. The literature's answer is to put the PD spring inside the solver as a compliant angular constraint (stiffness = 1/compliance) with damping applied as a clamped velocity pass (Müller 2020), or as a soft constraint parameterised by hertz and damping ratio (Catto). Both are implicit in the stiff term and stable at any gain.
- For a point-mass figure there is no body orientation, so the "hinge target angle" constraint becomes a three-particle angle constraint (parent, joint, child); the same XPBD multiplier update applies with the gradient of the angle with respect to the three positions, and the multiplier/h² gives the applied torque for logging or torque limits (clamping λ gives a torque-limited motor, analogous to AVBD's bound clamping).
- AVBD is attractive for a tiny 2D figure (about a dozen bodies, hard joints, 1/60 s, 4-5 iterations, MIT reference code in 2D), but it needs per-body 3x3 Hessian solves (2D rigid bodies) and warm-started per-constraint state, so it is a rewrite to rigid bodies rather than an increment on point masses. XPBD with substeps is the incremental path.
- Catto's precision warning (velocity from position differences) applies to any PBD code that keeps world-space positions in float32; in JS numbers are float64 unless Float32Array is used for state.

## 2. Contact and ground handling for a point-mass or capsule figure

### Takeaway
In the PBD family, ground contact is an inequality position constraint with friction handled either by clamping a tangential multiplier against μ times the normal multiplier (static, position pass) or by an explicit, clamped velocity update (dynamic, velocity pass); restitution and a cap on depenetration speed are needed because deriving velocity from positions otherwise turns penetration into pop. Engines converge on soft contacts plus speculative margins rather than hard clamps.

### Cited Findings
- **[read]** Static friction (position pass): compute the tangential relative motion of the contact points over the substep, Δp_t, and apply Δx = Δp_t with zero compliance "but only if λ_t < μ_s λ_n". — [Müller et al. 2020](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf)
- **[read]** Dynamic friction (velocity pass): Δv ← −(v_t/|v_t|) min(h μ_d |f_n|, |v_t|) with f_n = λ_n/h²; "The explicit form in connection with a Gauss-Seidel update allows us to make this step unconditionally stable", because the min guarantees the correction never exceeds the velocity itself. — [Müller et al. 2020](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf)
- **[read]** Restitution: record the pre-solve normal velocity v̄_n and, in the velocity pass, set the contact normal velocity to −e v̄_n. The same pass fixes "an important problem of PBD": velocities produced by the plain PBD velocity update are only a by-product of position correction, so initially overlapping objects get large separating velocities (marbles jump off the track with plain PBD, stay on with the velocity pass). — [Müller et al. 2020](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf)
- **[read]** Depenetration-speed cap at the position level: C = nᵀ(a−b) + max(d₀ − v_max Δtₛ, 0) ≥ 0. Small v_max separates gently; large v_max separates explosively; with no initial penetration it is a hard inequality. — [Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf)
- **[read]** Friction as attachment constraint with Coulomb clamp Δλ_f ← min(μ Δλ_n, Δλ_f), which "implicitly captures stick/slip transitions". — [Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf)
- **[read]** Broad phase once per frame, narrow phase per substep: collect candidate pairs once per time step using AABBs expanded by k Δt v_body (k = 2 in the paper's examples), then check actual collisions each substep with current geometry. Small Steps similarly generates contacts for features within a user margin so one detection per frame suffices. — [Müller et al. 2020](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf); [Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf)
- **[read]** AVBD contact: 3D constraint (2 friction + 1 normal) with multiplier bounds λ_n in [0, ∞) and tangential bounds ±μλ_n; static/dynamic switch from the previous step's cone test; a card tower held up purely by static friction is stable with hard constraints where VBD needs carefully tuned stiffness. — [AVBD paper](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/Augmented_VBD-SIGGRAPH25.pdf)
- **[search]** Box2D v3 "completely overhauls continuous collision and response. It uses a hybrid speculative and time of impact approach"; speculative collision "predicts contact points between fast moving objects before they touch. This allows the main physics step to prevent tunneling." — [Releasing Box2D 3.0](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/08/releasing-box2d-3.0); [Starting Box2D 3.0](https://box2d.org/posts/2023/01/starting-box2d-3.0/)
- **[read]** Box2D 3.2 (Oct 2026) adds "restitution iterations and propagation", contact recycling, per-body tunable continuous-collision safety factor, and continuous sensor events. — [v3.2.0 release listing](https://newreleases.io/project/github/erincatto/box2d/release/v3.2.0)
- **[read]** MuJoCo-based planar benchmarks rely on high foot friction: Walker2d-v5 sets friction 1.9 on both feet (v5 change made the two feet equal). — [Gymnasium Walker2d](https://gymnasium.farama.org/environments/mujoco/walker2d/)
- **[read]** In the minimal hybrid models used for teaching, ground contact is an instantaneous inelastic impact that conserves angular momentum about the new contact point (rimless wheel: θ̇⁺ = θ̇⁻ cos 2α; compass gait: floating-base impulse map then leg swap), with no slip and no double support. SLIP uses guards with no velocity jump at touchdown. — [Tedrake, Underactuated Robotics, ch. "Simple Models of Walking and Running"](https://underactuated.mit.edu/simple_legs.html)

### Inferences
- "Cheap all-body ground collision" for a point-mass figure against a flat (or height-field) ground is one inequality per particle: if y_i < ground(x_i) + r_i, project up along the normal, accumulate λ_n, and apply Müller-style friction. That is O(particles) per substep with no broad phase; with about a dozen particles the cost is negligible next to rod solves. Giving each particle a radius (head larger, hands smaller) turns points into circles for free; capsule limbs against flat ground reduce to their two end-circles, so no extra primitive is needed unless the terrain has corners sharper than limb length.
- Clamping only the feet (the current design) is a hard positional clamp with no friction model and no normal force, so there is no principled way to decide slip vs stick or to read ground reaction force. Keeping λ_n per contact gives both, and λ_n/h² is the ground reaction force that balance controllers (COP/ZMP estimates) need.
- The pop problem (penetration converted to launch velocity) is specific to "velocity from positions" schemes and gets worse as dt shrinks (v_sep = d₀/Δt). At 1/1440 s, a 1 mm penetration becomes 1.44 m/s of separating velocity unless capped; this is a likely source of foot jitter and a reason to add the v_max cap or a velocity-pass restitution of 0.
- Speculative contacts in the point-vs-ground case amount to creating the constraint when the predicted position (x + v dt) would cross the ground, which the inequality projection on predicted positions already does; a separate speculative margin only matters once collision detection is amortised across substeps.

## 3. Timestep independence

### Takeaway
The standard recipe is three independent layers: a fixed simulation step consumed by an accumulator with render interpolation; substeps rather than iterations inside that step; and either implicit/soft-constraint or closed-form integration for stiff spring-like terms so that stability does not depend on dt at all.

### Cited Findings
- **[read]** "Fix Your Timestep!" (Glenn Fiedler): simulate at constant dt; add frame time to an accumulator and consume it in dt-sized steps ("the renderer produces time and the simulation consumes it in discrete dt sized steps"); clamp the frame time (0.25 s in the final sample) or cap steps per frame to avoid the spiral of death; keep previous and current state and render the blend with alpha = accumulator/dt. Only a fully fixed step gives exact run-to-run reproducibility. — [Gaffer On Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/)
- **[read]** Position error from external forces scales with Δt², so substeps reduce error quadratically while iterations do not; the recommended extreme is maximum substeps with one iteration each. — [Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf); [Müller et al. 2020](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf)
- **[read]** With XPBD compliance, "forces and elongation are in the correct relation independent of the substep" and iteration count. — [Müller et al. 2020](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/PBDBodies.pdf)
- **[read]** Substep cost can be kept low by doing collision detection once per frame and reusing the contact set (Macklin), or storing contact points in body-local coordinates and updating separation analytically per substep (Catto). — [Small Steps](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf); [Solver2D post](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/02/solver2d/)
- **[read]** Soft constraints as damped harmonic oscillators (hertz, damping ratio) produce coefficients that depend on h explicitly, so the same spring behaves consistently across step sizes as long as hertz stays well under the Nyquist limit of the step rate. — [Solver2D post](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/02/solver2d/)
- **[read]** AVBD runs every example at 1/60 s with 4-5 iterations and fixed parameters, including hard joints and stacking, without substepping (substeps used only in one example for discrete collision detection). — [AVBD paper](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/Augmented_VBD-SIGGRAPH25.pdf)
- **[read]** Analytically integrated damped springs: a semi-implicit-Euler spring is "unstable" at large timesteps and can blow up for some stiffness/damping settings; the exact (closed-form) damped spring "produces a smooth, stable motion even with a very large dt or stiffness" and can evaluate the state at any future time directly. Critically damped form: with y = damping/2, j0 = x − c, j1 = v + j0·y: x' = e^(−y·dt)(j0 + j1·dt) + c, v' = e^(−y·dt)(v − j1·y·dt). Parameterising by half-life (damping = 4 ln2 / halflife) makes tuning dt-independent. Article dated 04/03/2021. — [The Orange Duck, "Spring-It-On: The Game Developer's Spring-Roll-Call"](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call)
- **[read]** MuJoCo planar benchmarks run physics at 0.002 s (500 Hz) with frame_skip 4, i.e. control at 125 Hz (Walker2d dt = 0.008 s). — [Gymnasium Walker2d](https://gymnasium.farama.org/environments/mujoco/walker2d/)
- **[read]** Spine's physics constraints are advanced by passing elapsed time via `Skeleton.update(deltaTime)` and a `Physics` mode argument to `updateWorldTransform` (e.g. `Physics.update`). — [Spine 4.2 blog](https://esotericsoftware.com/blog/Spine-4.2-The-physics-revolution)

### Inferences
- 1/1440 s is 24 substeps of a 60 Hz frame. By the standards above this is not unusual as a substep count (Müller 2020 uses 20-40 substeps x 1 iteration), but combined with 8 iterations it is about 8x the solver work those papers would spend. The correctness dependence on exactly 1/1440 means behaviour is tuned to the step (PBD stiffness, explicit torque gains, damping expressed per step). Making the library "dt-parametric" requires: (a) XPBD compliance for rods/springs, (b) implicit or closed-form PD for joint drives, (c) damping expressed as rates (per second or half-life) with exp(−rate·dt) rather than per-step multipliers, (d) contact with capped depenetration speed.
- With those four changes, the outer loop can be the Fiedler accumulator at, say, 1/120 s or 1/60 s frame step with N substeps chosen by budget, and results at different N converge to the same motion instead of to different motions.
- For a library consumer, render interpolation (alpha blend of previous/current particle positions) is cheap for a point-mass figure, since the state is just positions; no slerp is needed in 2D.
- Closed-form springs are directly usable for the non-physical, "procedural" layers (target smoothing, secondary motion, camera) and guarantee dt independence there; they cannot directly replace joint PD inside a coupled multi-body solve, where implicit/XPBD formulation is the equivalent.

### Gaps
- No primary source was read this session on Stable PD (Tan, Liu, Turk 2011) or on implicit spring integration (Baraff-Witkin); Small Steps only cites Tan et al. as using the predicted next-step state for PD. The claim that implicit PD is the standard fix for explicit-torque instability is therefore my inference plus that citation.
- I found no published measurement of how large a dt an XPBD point-mass biped with compliant angle constraints tolerates; that would need an experiment.

## 4. Planar biped models in research and teaching; open-source JS walkers

### Takeaway

- Engine joint motors (Rapier's stiffness/damping position motor; Box2D 3.2's motor joint with spring and velocity targets) are implicit PD drives solved inside the constraint solver. They are complementary output targets (drive a Spine/Rive rig from a simulated skeleton) rather than alternatives to the simulator.

## 6. Differentiable / accelerated simulators and derivative-free tuning for offline controller optimisation

### Takeaway
In 2026 the Python ecosystem has consolidated around MuJoCo: MJX (JAX) is the differentiable path, MuJoCo Warp is the fast GPU path but is not differentiable, Brax has retreated to being a training library, and Newton 1.0 (March 2026) packages MuJoCo Warp, VBD and other solvers on NVIDIA Warp. For low-dimensional controller parameters, derivative-free search (CMA-ES) on the real simulator is the established precedent, and no mature JS/WebGPU differentiable physics stack was found.

### Cited Findings
- **[search]** Brax: per its PyPI page, "only brax/training is actively being maintained as of 0.13.0"; users are told to use MuJoCo Playground instead of `brax/envs` and MJX or MuJoCo Warp for physics rather than Brax as a wrapper. MuJoCo's docs say Brax's generalized pipeline will be deprecated in favour of MJX. — [brax on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/brax/); [MJX docs](https://mujoco.org/book/mjx.html); [Brax discussion #409](https://www.github.com/google/brax/discussions/409)
- **[search]** MJX (JAX): "The dynamics API in MJX is automatically differentiable via JAX." — [MJX docs](https://mujoco.org/book/mjx.html)
- **[read]** MuJoCo Warp (MJWarp): MuJoCo re-implemented in NVIDIA Warp, jointly maintained by NVIDIA and Google DeepMind (`pip install mujoco-warp`), optimised for parallel throughput (RL); MuJoCo proper remains better for latency-sensitive use such as MPC or interactive viewers because a single MJWarp step is slower. ### Inferences
- Tuning in an external simulator (MJX, Newton) and deploying in the bespoke TypeScript simulator introduces a sim-to-sim gap: a point-mass/rod PBD figure with foot clamps does not have the same dynamics as a MuJoCo planar walker with soft contacts. Parameters tuned offline would transfer only as initial guesses unless the TS model is re-implemented faithfully on the other side (for example as a JAX or Warp port of the same XPBD step, which is small enough to be practical and would then be differentiable or batched for free).
- For a controller with tens of parameters (PD gains, phase timings, foot-placement gains), the SIMBICON-optimisation precedent suggests CMA-ES directly on the real TS simulator, run headless in Node or Web Workers, is the lowest-friction option: no gradients, no model mismatch, and the simulator is cheap enough to run many rollouts (section 7).
- Gradients through contact-rich biped rollouts are known to be poorly conditioned; the fact that the highest-throughput 2026 simulator (MJWarp) ships without differentiability and is recommended for RL throughput suggests the field's centre of gravity for locomotion is sampling/RL rather than analytic gradients. This is an inference from product positioning, not a cited claim.
- No evidence was found of a production-ready differentiable rigid-body engine in JS/WebGPU. If gradients are wanted in-browser, the realistic route is hand-derived or autodiff (Rose-style) gradients of the author's own tiny step function.

### Gaps
- Not researched this session (no sources fetched): Nimble, Dojo, Tiny Differentiable Simulator, Bayesian optimisation for gait/controller tuning, jax-js or taichi.js browser status. No claims are made about them.
- Exact current versions of MJX, MuJoCo Warp and Newton as of November 2026 were not confirmed (MuJoCo docs exist for 3.10.0; Newton announced a monthly cadence after 1.0).
- MJWarp differentiability status may have changed after the docs page was written; I did not open GitHub issue 500.
- Whether Genesis rigid-body differentiability has shipped is unconfirmed.
- No benchmark was found comparing CMA-ES against gradient-based tuning on a planar biped controller.

## 7. Bespoke tiny simulator vs. adopting an engine for character control

### Takeaway
I found no direct, quantitative study of this trade-off for JS character controllers. The available evidence is indirect: engines now offer determinism and (partly experimental) snapshots, sampling-based MPC needs only tens of rollouts per control step when the simulator is fast, and a point-mass XPBD figure is so small that its state is a handful of typed arrays, which is the property look-ahead rollouts depend on most.

### Cited Findings
- **[search]** MuJoCo MPC (MJPC, "Predictive Sampling: Real-time Behaviour Synthesis with MuJoCo", arXiv 2212.00541) runs predictive control in real time with planners (gradient descent, iLQG, predictive sampling) in C++ that "extensively utilise multi-threading for parallel rollouts"; predictive sampling is described as a simple zero-order baseline that is surprisingly competitive. — [arXiv 2212.00541](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.00541)
- **[search]** CPU-thread count bounds sampling MPC on MuJoCo ("constrained by the number of CPU threads, limiting real-time performance when many samples are required"); a 2025 paper reports real-time control on standard CPU hardware "with as few as 30 samples". — [arXiv 2307.09105](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.09105v3); [arXiv 2512.21109](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21109v1)
- **[read]** MuJoCo Warp's docs recommend classic MuJoCo for "latency-sensitive work like model predictive control", since batched GPU simulators have slower single steps. — [MJWarp docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/mjwarp/index.html)
- **[read]** Rapier JS: cross-platform bit-determinism under stated conditions and whole-world snapshots via `takeSnapshot()`; JS-side transcendental functions are a determinism hazard. — [Rapier determinism guide](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/determinism)
- **[read]** Box2D 3.2: snapshot/restore is "experimental" and "more efficient rollback" is listed as future work, i.e. rollback is not yet a tuned fast path in October 2026. — [Box2D 3.2 post](https://box2d.org/posts/2026/10/box2d-3.2/); [v3.2.0 release listing](https://newreleases.io/project/github/erincatto/box2d/release/v3.2.0)
- **[search]** Catto: rollback "is only reliable if all your game code is deterministic"; rollback can leave entity IDs orphaned that the application must manage. — [Replay post](https://box2d.org/posts/2026/06/replay/)
- **[read]** MuJoCo WASM bindings require explicit `.delete()` on every Embind object, some properties are copies that go stale after stepping, and multithreading needs cross-origin isolation headers. — [mujoco/wasm README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/main/wasm/README.md)
- **[read]** Engine authors themselves treat solver choice as empirical and scene-dependent: Catto tuned Soft Step "on average across samples", and reports that XPBD gave him friction and precision trouble in a general-purpose engine. — [Solver2D post](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/02/solver2d/)
- **[read]** Single-file browser JS simulators of pendulums, rigid bodies and XPBD joints are an established teaching format (Ten Minute Physics, 25 tutorials). — [Ten Minute Physics](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/tenMinutePhysics/index.html)
- **[read]** A fully fixed step is the precondition for exact reproducibility. — [Fix Your Timestep!](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/)

### Inferences
- Arguments for staying bespoke, given the evidence: (1) state cloning is a typed-array copy of roughly a dozen particles' positions/velocities plus controller state, versus whole-world snapshot serialisation in Rapier or experimental snapshots in Box2D; (2) determinism is trivially under the author's control (single thread, fixed order, float64) as long as the fixed step is kept and platform-variant `Math` functions are avoided or accepted; (3) zero bundle and init cost; (4) the solver can be specialised (flat ground, no broad phase, fixed topology).
- Arguments for an engine: implicit joint motors, friction, restitution, CCD and general shape collision are already solved and tuned at 60 Hz; the bespoke code currently lacks all of these and needs 1440 Hz. The research in sections 1-3 indicates the gap is closable with a few hundred lines (XPBD compliance, angle constraints with compliance, velocity pass for friction/damping/restitution, per-particle ground inequality), which weakens the case for adopting a multi-hundred-kB WASM dependency in a library whose selling point is being dependency-free.
- Rollout budget arithmetic (my estimate, not a measurement): if a step costs on the order of 1 iteration over about 15-20 constraints and a dozen particles, moving from 1440 Hz x 8 iterations to, for example, 240-480 Hz x 1 iteration cuts cost per simulated second by roughly 24x-48x. That is the difference between a few and a few hundred half-second look-ahead rollouts per frame in single-threaded JS, and MJPC-style results suggest tens of samples can already be useful. The fixed 1/1440 s step is therefore the main obstacle to rollout-based control, more than the choice of bespoke vs engine.
- A hybrid is available: keep the bespoke simulator for runtime and rollouts, and use an engine (MuJoCo planar model via MJX or `@mujoco/mujoco`) only as an offline cross-check of gaits, accepting the model gap.

### Gaps
- No published benchmark of rollouts-per-frame for JS physics (bespoke or engine) in a character-control setting was found; the numbers above are estimates and should be replaced by measurement.
- No source quantifies snapshot/restore cost in rapier.js or Box2D WASM ports.
- No case study was found of a shipped browser game or library choosing between a custom 2D ragdoll simulator and an engine for active-ragdoll control; QWOP-like games are the obvious examples but their engine choices were not verified.
- MJPC's specific rollout counts and horizon for the planar walker task were not retrieved.
