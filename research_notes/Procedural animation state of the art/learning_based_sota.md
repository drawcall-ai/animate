# Learning-based state of the art in character animation and physics-based character control (as of November 2026)

Reader notes:
- Items tagged **[2025-26]** are recent work; **[foundation]** marks pre-2025 work the field builds on.
- Items tagged **(not re-verified)** are stated from the researcher's background knowledge with a canonical URL, but the page was not opened in this session. Everything else was read from the cited page in this session (some only via abstract pages; where a number came from a search-result summary rather than the paper itself, this is said).
- Almost all work below is 3D humanoid. Evidence on 2D/planar characters is thin and is isolated in the last section.

## 1. Physics-based imitation and skill learning: what is the current best "universal" physics-based humanoid controller, and what can it do?

### Takeaway
The dominant recipe since 2023 is: (1) train a large motion-tracking policy with RL on thousands of mocap clips in a GPU simulator, then (2) distil it into a generative or latent-conditioned controller (PULSE latent space, MaskedMimic masked inpainting, FSQ tokens in SONIC) that higher-level inputs (text, keyframes, VR, joystick, a kinematic planner) can drive. As of late 2026 the largest published instance is NVIDIA's SONIC (42M parameters, 100M+ frames, up to 21k GPU-hours), with MaskedMimic still the reference "unified" controller in the animation literature; none of these are small-scale methods.

### Cited Findings

**Foundations (2018-2024)**
- [foundation] DeepMimic (Peng, Abbeel, Levine, van de Panne; ACM TOG / SIGGRAPH 2018) showed standard RL can train simulated characters to imitate individual mocap clips, combining a motion-imitation objective with a task objective; demonstrated tasks include walking in a desired direction and throwing a ball at a user-specified target; characters include a human, Atlas, a bipedal dinosaur and a dragon. — [DeepMimic project page](https://xbpeng.github.io/projects/DeepMimic/index.html)
- [foundation] AMP (Peng et al., SIGGRAPH 2021) replaces the hand-written tracking reward with an adversarial discriminator that scores motion style, so the policy matches the distribution of a dataset rather than a specific clip (not re-verified). — [AMP arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02180)
- [foundation] ASE (Peng, Guo, Halper, Levine, Fidler; 2022) combines adversarial imitation with unsupervised RL to learn reusable skill embeddings from unstructured clips; trained with "over a decade of simulated experiences" in a massively parallel GPU simulator. — [ASE arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01906)
- [foundation] CALM (Tessler et al., SIGGRAPH 2023) learns a conditional latent motion space that can be directed by a high-level policy or FSM (not re-verified); it is still used as the low-level embedding model in 2025 work such as PhysicsFC. — [CALM arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02195); usage confirmed in [PhysicsFC](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21216)
- [foundation] PHC (Luo et al., ICCV 2023): a single tracking controller imitates 98.9% of AMASS training sequences (11,313 sequences; 96.4% on test) without external forces; trained about one week on one NVIDIA A100 with roughly 10 billion samples in Isaac Gym (policy 30 Hz, sim 60 Hz); networks are MLPs of [1024, 512]; the four-primitive model is 28.8 MB; a recovery primitive gets back up from falls in 98.8% of cases within 10 s. — [PHC arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.06456)
- [foundation] PULSE (Luo et al., ICLR 2024): distils PHC+ (100% success on cleaned AMASS-train, 99.2% on test) into a 32-dimensional latent space with a proprioception-conditioned learned prior; about 10^10 samples for PHC+ and 10^9 for PULSE; downstream tasks include speed, strike, reach, terrain traversal and VR-controller tracking. — [PULSE arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.04582)
- [foundation] MaskedMimic (Tessler, Guo, Nabati, Chechik, Peng; ACM TOG / SIGGRAPH Asia 2024) frames physics-based control as motion inpainting: one model generates motion from partial/masked descriptions — masked keyframes, objects, text, scene information or combinations — avoiding per-behaviour reward engineering. — [MaskedMimic arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14393)
- MaskedMimic's demonstrated interfaces: full-body and sparse tracking (e.g. head-only or head-and-hands), locomotion via head targets, hand reach to an (x,y,z) target with a time limit, steering via pelvis pose, text-only prompts ("balances on one foot"), object interaction, text-stylised path following; terrain and unseen-object generalisation shown in video; code in NVlabs/ProtoMotions. The project page reports no success-rate numbers. — [MaskedMimic project page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/par/projects/maskedmimic/)
- A later paper describes MaskedMimic as a masked cVAE distilled from a tracking policy, and criticises it for lacking planning and not generalising to unseen control signals. — [UniPhys, ICCV 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12540)

**2025-2026**
- [2025-26] SONIC (Luo, Yuan, Wang, ... Zhu; arXiv Nov 2025, published Science Robotics 11(117), 2026) scales motion tracking as the single training objective: network 1.2M to 42M parameters, 100M+ frames from 700 hours of mocap, 21k GPU-hours. — [SONIC arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07820)
- SONIC details: trained in Isaac Lab, 128 GPUs for 7 days (scaling runs at 16/32/128 GPUs = about 2k/9k/21k GPU-hours); MLP encoders/decoders with a finite scalar quantiser producing motion tokens; policy at 50 Hz; 1-2 ms per forward pass on a Jetson Orin; simulation success 98.7% / 99.6% / 97.0% on three test sets versus 81.6% / 85.8% / 73.4% for BeyondMimic; 99.2% real-world success over 123 sequences on a Unitree G1; interfaces include a real-time kinematic planner driven by gamepad (0-6 m/s commands, tracker achieves up to about 4 m/s; styles, boxing, crouch, crawl), video teleoperation, text and music conditioning via a generator, VR, and a vision-language-action model (GR00T N1.5). Stated limitations: no formal treatment of safety/energy; can lose balance on very dynamic motions. — [SONIC paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07820v3)
- SONIC was open-sourced on 2026-02-19 (checkpoints, C++ inference stack, VR teleop); code Apache 2.0, weights under NVIDIA Open Model License (from search-result summary of the model card). — [GEAR-SONIC on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/GEAR-SONIC)
- Conflict: one summary of SONIC quotes "9k GPU hours" and another "21k"; the paper HTML shows both are scaling tiers (32 vs 128 GPUs), with 21k the largest. — [SONIC paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07820v3); [SONIC project page](https://nvlabs.github.io/GEAR-SONIC/)
- [2025-26] ADD (Zhang, Bashkirov, Yang, Shi, Taylor, Peng; SIGGRAPH Asia 2025; SFU / Sony PlayStation / NVIDIA) replaces the hand-weighted sum of tracking reward terms with an adversarial "differential" discriminator whose only positive sample is the zero vector (zero difference between agent state and reference state), removing manual reward tuning. — [ADD project page](https://xbpeng.github.io/projects/ADD/index.html); [ADD arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04961)
- [2025-26] MimicKit (Peng; arXiv Oct 2025, v4 Jan 2026) is an open-source framework implementing DeepMimic, AMP, ASE, ADD and AWR, supporting Isaac Gym, Isaac Lab and Newton; example training uses 4096 parallel environments. Reported humanoid tracking errors (position m; AMP / DeepMimic / ADD): run 0.163 / 0.013 / 0.165; backflip 0.267 / 0.111 / 0.062; spin-kick 0.064 / 0.078 / 0.025 — AMP tracks poorly by design, DeepMimic and ADD track accurately. — [MimicKit arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13794v4)
- [2025-26] PARC (Xu, Shi, Yin, Peng; SIGGRAPH 2025) iterates between a kinematic motion generator and a physics-based tracking controller: the generator proposes terrain-traversal motions for new terrains, the tracker "physically corrects" them in simulation, and the corrected motions are added back to the dataset; yields parkour combinations absent from the original data (e.g. jump-then-climb). — [PARC project page](https://xbpeng.github.io/projects/PARC/index.html); [PARC arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04002)
- [2025-26] ProtoMotions3 (NVIDIA) is the open framework hosting MaskedMimic; backends listed are Newton (beta), Isaac Gym, Isaac Lab, Genesis and MuJoCo (CPU). Secondary/mirrored pages claim a general tracking policy trained on the full BONES-SEED dataset (about 142K motions) transferred zero-shot to a Unitree G1, and AMASS (40+ hours) learned within 12 hours on 4 A100s — these two figures were seen only in search summaries and are unverified. — [ProtoMotions GitHub](https://github.com/NVlabs/ProtoMotions); [ProtoMotions docs](https://nvlabs.github.io/ProtoMotions)
- [2025-26] MaskedManipulator extends MaskedMimic's masking scheme to include object goals for loco-manipulation (seen only via a third-party review; venue/date unverified). — [Moonlight review of MaskedManipulator](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/maskedmanipulator-versatile-whole-body-control-for-loco-manipulation)
- [2025-26] Other 2025 tracking-centric humanoid controllers named in the literature: BeyondMimic (tracking policies distilled into a state-action diffusion model, guided at test time), UniTracker, GMT, Any2Track. — [BeyondMimic arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08241v3); [UniTracker arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.07356); baselines listed in [SONIC paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07820v3)

### Inferences
- "Universal controller" in 2026 means "near-100% tracking of large mocap corpora plus a learned interface on top". The animation-side reference is MaskedMimic (2024); the robotics-side scale leader is SONIC (2025-26). No single paper found in this session was shown to supersede MaskedMimic across all of text/keyframe/object/scene conditioning for animation characters; newer work (UniPhys, MIND, SONIC) beats it on specific axes.
- Every method in this family depends on (a) mocap data (hours to hundreds of hours), (b) a GPU-parallel simulator with thousands of environments, and (c) 10^9-10^10 simulation samples. None of this transfers directly to a dependency-free browser library.
- The transferable *ideas* are structural, not the trained artefacts: reference-state initialisation and early termination (DeepMimic lineage), tracking-then-distil, separating a low-level "stay upright and track" layer from a high-level "what to do" layer, and ADD's observation that a single learned/aggregated error signal can replace many hand-weighted reward terms. For a hand-written controller, the analogous lesson is to keep a robust low-level tracking/balance layer and drive it with simple kinematic targets.

### Gaps
- MaskedMimic's quantitative success rates and training compute were not obtained (abstract and project page give none).
- No confirmed "MaskedMimic 2" from the original authors was found; a search specifically for one returned nothing.
- AMP/ASE/CALM training hours and environment counts were not re-read from the papers in this session.
- No dedicated 2025-2026 survey of physics-based character control was found; the most-cited broad survey remains the 2022 Eurographics STAR ([arXiv 2203.04735](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.04735.pdf)).

## 2. Physics-based diffusion and generative control (PDP, CLoSD, DiffuseLoco, UniPhys, MIND, token priors)

### Takeaway
Two designs coexist: "kinematic diffusion planner + RL tracker in a closed loop" (CLoSD, Kimodo+PHC) and "end-to-end diffusion over actions/states cloned from RL experts" (PDP, UniPhys, BeyondMimic, MIND). By mid-2026 end-to-end text-driven physics control has roughly closed much of the gap to kinematic text-to-motion on benchmark metrics, but diffusion inference speed (about 10-20 FPS in UniPhys) and falls on dynamic motions remain the stated limits.

### Cited Findings
- [foundation] PDP (2024): train expert RL policies, roll them out with action noise, and behaviour-clone a diffusion policy on "noisy-state, clean-action" pairs. That sampling choice is decisive: 93.5% tracking success versus 68.8% for clean-state data, and 100% versus 3.36% on perturbation recovery. Text-to-motion succeeds on 57.1% of 42 prompts (11.9% for an MLP). Training: 1 RTX 2080 Ti for about 1.5 h (perturbation task) up to 8 V100s for about 70 h (AMASS tracking). Limitations: K-step denoising is K times slower than an MLP; text-to-motion lags kinematic models because balance maintenance disrupts the requested motion. — [PDP arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.00960)
- [foundation] CLoSD (Tevet, Raab, Cohan, Reda, Luo, Peng, Bermano, van de Panne; arXiv Oct 2024): a fast autoregressive diffusion planner (DiP), conditioned on text and target locations, feeds short motion plans to an RL tracking controller which returns simulated state — a closed loop. Tasks: goal navigation, striking an object with a named hand or foot, sitting, getting up, and chaining these. — [CLoSD arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03441)
- [foundation] DiffuseLoco (2024) trains a diffusion policy for legged locomotion from offline multi-skill datasets and deploys it in real time on robots (not re-verified). — [DiffuseLoco arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19264)
- [2025-26] UniPhys (Wu et al., ICCV 2025): diffusion-forcing behaviour cloning where each token holds a latent action plus the resulting next state, unifying planner and controller. 12-layer causal transformer, hidden 768; data = 4,875 text-annotated BABEL sequences (15.7 h) produced by tracking AMASS with PULSE; Isaac Gym; about 10 GPU-days on one GPU. Text alignment "correct": UniPhys 56.3%, CLoSD 61.6%, MaskedMimic 42.9%; jerk 1.2 vs 3.4 (CLoSD) vs 4.2 (MaskedMimic). Goal reaching (walk-far): UniPhys 0.95, CLoSD 1.0, MaskedMimic 0.0. Inference about 10 FPS (autoregressive) or 18 FPS (gradual schedule). Limitations: slower than RL policies, still falls on dynamic actions, no terrain, no hands. — [UniPhys arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12540)
- [2025-26] Diffuse-CLoC (arXiv Mar 2025, v3 Aug 2025): guided diffusion for physics-based look-ahead control; argues earlier diffusion control policies lack kinematic prediction and hence steerability (from search summary). — [Diffuse-CLoC arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11801v3)
- [2025-26] BeyondMimic (arXiv Aug 2025): three stages — train sim-to-real tracking policies with one MDP and one hyperparameter set, distil into a state-action diffusion model, deploy; joystick teleoperation and obstacle avoidance are achieved by guidance at inference without retraining. — [BeyondMimic arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08241v3)
- [2025-26] MIND (Li, Zhang, Liang et al.; arXiv May 2026, listed for TOG): end-to-end text-to-action diffusion with an "intent" VAE latent (downsampling 4, dim 32), a sequence-level and a step-level intent predictor, and an action diffusion transformer; trained on HumanML3D motions tracked with PHC; Isaac Gym, SMPL-like humanoid with 69 actuators; 8 RTX 4090s for about 12 hours. HumanML3D-test results (R-precision top-1 / FID): PDP 0.032 / 1.62; CLoSD 0.172 / 1.54; UniPhys 0.087 / 0.60; Kimodo++ (kinematic generator + PHC+ tracker) 0.386 / 0.268; MIND 0.468 / 0.118. Limitations: crawling and "reach for toes" often fall; short-to-medium horizons only; weak on compositional language; diffusion speed limits real-time use. MaskedMimic is not in MIND's quantitative comparison. — [MIND arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26006)
- [2025-26] Other 2025-26 works MIND lists in this space: SMP (reusable score-matching motion priors), InterAgent (multi-agent diffusion), Sentinel (end-to-end language-action), TextOp (real-time interactive text-driven humanoid robot control, 2026), PhyGile (2026), HIL (parkour from video). — [MIND arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26006)
- [2025-26] A 2025 two-stage in-betweening method pairs a character-agnostic diffusion model with physics-based adaptation. — [Scalable Motion In-betweening arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09413)
- Token/VQ priors: SONIC's action space is FSQ tokens (2 tokens, 32 levels, 32 dims); using tokens rather than SMPL poses as the VLA action space gave 68% vs 27% average task success. — [SONIC paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07820v3)

### Inferences
- All of these are behaviour-cloned from RL trackers, so they inherit the full cost of section 1 plus transformer training. The one clearly reusable small-scale insight is PDP's "noisy-state, clean-action" data collection: if a hand-written controller already works, perturbing the state and recording the controller's corrective output is what makes a cloned policy robust.
- The planner/tracker split (CLoSD) mirrors what a procedural library can do without learning: a cheap kinematic "intent" generator (target poses, foot placements) re-planned every few frames from the simulated state, tracked by a PD/balance layer.

### Gaps
- CLoSD's quantitative results, planner frame rate, and final venue were not obtained (abstract page only).
- DiffuseLoco details not re-read. Diffuse-CLoC and SMP details come from summaries only.

## 3. Kinematic generation: motion matching, phase networks, diffusion, text-to-motion leaders and benchmarks

### Takeaway
Interactive kinematic control in games is still anchored in motion matching and its learned compressions; research leaders for offline text-to-motion in 2026 are large diffusion / flow / masked-token models, with HumanML3D widely regarded as small and saturated and industrial-scale models (NVIDIA Kimodo, 700 h of studio mocap, 282M parameters) moving off it.

### Cited Findings
**Interactive / game-oriented**
- [foundation] Learned Motion Matching (Holden, Kanoun, Perepichka, Popa; Ubisoft La Forge, SIGGRAPH 2020) trains three specialised networks to replace three components of motion matching so only network weights, not the animation database, are kept in memory; memory stays small as data grows. — [The Orange Duck: Learned Motion Matching](https://www.theorangeduck.com/page/learned-motion-matching); [Ubisoft La Forge](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/studio/laforge/news/3NWqwcMU9lMfumgfPAEfka/learned-motion-matching)
- [foundation] Phase-Functioned NN (Holden 2017), Mode-Adaptive NN (Zhang, Starke 2018) and later Starke models are autoregressive next-pose predictors gated by a phase variable or learned gating (not re-verified). — [PFNN paper page](https://theorangeduck.com/page/phase-functioned-neural-networks-character-control)
- [foundation] DeepPhase (Starke, Mason, Komura; SIGGRAPH 2022, Best in Show technical paper award) introduces the Periodic Autoencoder, which learns periodic phase features from large unstructured motion data without supervision. — [SIGGRAPH history: award](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=214346); [DeepPhase paper](https://i.cs.hku.hk/~taku/deepphase.pdf)
- [foundation] WalkTheDog (Li, Starke, Ye, Sorkine-Hornung; SIGGRAPH 2024) uses a vector-quantised periodic autoencoder with a shared codebook to align motions across morphologies (human and dog) on a common 1D phase manifold, paired with an improved motion-matching framework. — [WalkTheDog paper](https://peizhuoli.github.io/walkthedog/papers/walk-the-dog-camera-ready-with-supp.pdf)
- [foundation] Categorical Codebook Matching (ACM TOG 2024, attributed in search results to He/Starke-adjacent authors with Komura and Ye) matches distributions between input and output categorical codebooks for responsive VR/game character synthesis (from search summary; authorship not verified). — [HKU repository entry](https://repository.hku.hk/handle/10722/362417)
- [2025-26] "Control Operators for Interactive Character Animation" (Gou, van de Panne, Holden; Epic Games / UBC; ACM TOG, Dec 2025) demonstrates control encodings on two "current state-of-the-art interactive character controllers": a flow-matching autoregressive model and a variant of Learned Motion Matching (from search summary; PDF too large to fetch). — [Control Operators PDF](https://theorangeduck.com/media/uploads/other_stuff/ControlOperators.pdf)
- [2025-26] A 2025 Computer Graphics Forum paper addresses making learned motion matching stable and fast using Lipschitz-continuous networks and sparse mixture-of-experts (title-level evidence only, from search summary). — [search result context via Ubisoft La Forge page](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/studio/laforge/news/3NWqwcMU9lMfumgfPAEfka/learned-motion-matching)

**Offline text-to-motion**
- [foundation] MDM (Tevet et al., ICLR 2023) is the reference transformer motion diffusion model; MoMask (Guo et al., CVPR 2024) uses residual VQ tokens with masked generative transformers (not re-verified). — [MDM arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14916); [MoMask arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00063)
- HumanML3D: 29,024 motions with 87,834 text descriptions, 20 FPS, clips up to 10 s, sourced from AMASS and HumanAct12. — [MoLingo arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13840v2)
- [2025-26] MoLingo (arXiv Dec 2025, revised Jul 2026): autoregressive rectified-flow model in a semantically aligned latent space. HumanML3D under the MARDM-67 evaluation protocol (FID / R-precision top-1): real data 0.000 / 0.503; MDM (50 steps) 0.518 / 0.440; MLD 0.431 / 0.461; MoMask 0.116 / 0.490; MoMask++ 0.108 / 0.492; MoGenTS 0.060 / 0.500; DisCoRD 0.053 / 0.506; ACMDM-XL 0.058 / 0.522; MoLingo (VAE) 0.049 / 0.528; MoLingo (SAE) 0.066 / 0.544. Limitation: body only, no detailed hands. — [MoLingo arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13840v2)
- Several top models now score above the ground truth on R-precision (real = 0.503 top-1 vs MoLingo 0.544). — [MoLingo arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13840v2)
- [2025-26] Other 2026 self-reported HumanML3D SOTA claims: RAM / Reconstruction-Anchored Diffusion (Jan 2026; R@1 56.1%, FID 0.032 with 20 steps), CDAMD (Apr 2026), MotionHiFlow (Apr 2026). These use differing evaluators and are not directly comparable to the MoLingo table (figures from search summaries). — [RAM alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.14788v1); [MotionHiFlow arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23264v1)
- [2025-26] Kimodo (Rempe et al., NVIDIA; arXiv Mar 2026): kinematic diffusion with a two-stage (root then body) transformer denoiser, 282M parameters (S/M/L = 56M/148M/282M), trained on Bones Rigplay — about 700 hours of optical mocap from 170 subjects with human-written text — on 16 A100-80GB GPUs. Supports text plus full-body keyframes, sparse end-effector constraints, 2D root waypoints, dense 2D paths and foot-contact patterns. Reported constraint errors about 2.7-3.1 cm. Inference about 2-5 s per prompt on an RTX 3090; explicitly designed for offline authoring, not real time. Limitations: 10 s clips, no scene/object interaction, real-time autoregressive use is future work; demo uses post-processing (foot locking, IK). — [Kimodo arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15546)
- Kimodo's authors argue HumanML3D is small (about 30 hours) and saturated, with recent methods reporting better-than-ground-truth scores, and that the usual retrieval-within-batches-of-32 protocol is weak. — [Kimodo arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15546)
- Public Kimodo variants are trained on the BONES-SEED dataset (one model card lists 288 hours); checkpoints exist for the SOMA body model and the Unitree G1 robot (from search summary of project page/model cards). — [Kimodo project page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/sil/projects/kimodo)
- [2025-26] Motion-X++ (arXiv Jan 2025): 180.9 hours of whole-body motion, 120.5K sequences, 19.5M frame-level pose descriptions, with text, audio and video; the original Motion-X had 81.1K sequences and 15.6M pose annotations. — [Motion-X++ arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.05098)

### Inferences
- For a real-time, interactive, tiny-footprint setting, the relevant kinematic ideas are the old ones — motion matching (nearest-neighbour search in a feature database) and phase variables — not diffusion. Diffusion text-to-motion models are hundreds of millions of parameters and seconds per clip.
- Phase as an explicit state variable (PFNN → DeepPhase) is directly portable to a hand-written controller: gait cycles, swing/stance timing and blending can be indexed by a phase without any network.
- Kinematic generators plus physics trackers are being used as each other's data engines (PARC, Kimodo++/PHC+), which signals that kinematic output alone is still not trusted to be physically valid.

### Gaps
- Learned Motion Matching's exact memory/runtime numbers and the three network names were not retrieved (PDF exceeded fetch size).
- No confirmed list of shipped 2025-2026 games using learned motion matching or neural animation controllers was found.
- Details of the Control Operators flow-matching controller (size, ms/frame) were not retrieved.
- No independent, protocol-controlled leaderboard for HumanML3D / Motion-X in 2026 was found; all rankings are self-reported.

## 4. Humanoid "foundation" / behaviour models from robotics and their relevance to animation

### Takeaway
Robotics has converged on the same tracking-then-distil recipe as animation and now publishes at larger scale (SONIC); "behavioural foundation models" (Meta Motivo, BFM-Zero) add zero-shot prompting by reward, goal pose or motion clip. Their relevance to animation is that they are the same technology with sim-to-real constraints added; their relevance to a tiny 2D library is conceptual only.

### Cited Findings
- [foundation] HOVER (NVIDIA et al., 2024): an oracle motion imitator trained on retargeted AMASS is distilled with DAgger into one student policy whose command space (kinematic positions, joint angles, root tracking) is selected by a mask — "over 15 useful modes"; Unitree H1 (19 DOF), Isaac Gym; beats multi-mode RL baseline on 32/32 metric-mode comparisons. — [HOVER arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21229)
- [foundation] HumanPlus (Stanford, 2024): humanoid shadowing of human motion from an RGB camera plus imitation learning of skills (not re-verified). — [HumanPlus arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10454)
- [2025-26] Meta Motivo (FAIR; "Zero-Shot Whole-Body Humanoid Control via Behavioral Foundation Models", arXiv Apr 2025): algorithm FB-CPR regularises forward-backward unsupervised RL toward unlabeled mocap; trained on 8,902 AMASS motions (about 29 h) in MuJoCo (450 Hz sim, 30 Hz control, SMPL humanoid, 69-D actions), 30M interaction steps; base model about 25M parameters, largest about 288M. At test time it is prompted zero-shot by a reward function, a goal pose or a motion to track: about 73.4% of task-specific top-line performance overall, about 61% of per-reward TD3 on reward tasks, 83% tracking success on test motions; rated more human-like than pure reward-optimised policies in 83% of comparisons. Limitations: unnatural falling/standing, struggles far from mocap data, proprioception only (no navigation/objects), no language. Code and models released. — [Meta Motivo arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11054v1)
- [2025-26] BFM-Zero (Li, Luo et al., with Meta co-authors; arXiv Nov 2025): a promptable behavioural foundation model on a real Unitree G1 using off-policy unsupervised RL; zero-shot reward optimisation, goal reaching and motion tracking, plus few-shot adaptation; trained in Isaac Lab. Described by its authors as the first BFM validated on a real humanoid (from search summary). — [BFM-Zero arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04131); [BFM-Zero project page](https://lecar-lab.github.io/BFM-Zero)
- [2025-26] "Behavior Foundation Model for Humanoid Robots" (arXiv Jun 2025, revised) is a separate generative BFM pretrained on large behavioural datasets. — [BFM arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20487v5)
- [2025-26] SONIC is positioned as the low-level whole-body "system 1" under NVIDIA's GR00T VLA: GR00T N1.5 outputs a 78-dim action (64-dim motion token + 14 hand joints); average 75% success over six loco-manipulation tasks (60-95% per task). — [SONIC paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07820v3); docs at [GR00T-WholeBodyControl](https://nvlabs.github.io/GR00T-WholeBodyControl/)
- [2025-26] Kimodo ships a Unitree G1 variant, i.e. the kinematic text-to-motion generator and the robot tracker share a pipeline. — [Kimodo project page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/sil/projects/kimodo)
- [2025-26] A Feb 2026 arXiv paper reports basketball and football skills (dribbling, layups) on a Unitree G1 (title-level, from search summary). — [arXiv 2602.02473](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.02473)

### Inferences
- Animation and humanoid robotics are now one research community for whole-body control (shared authors: Luo, Peng, Tessler, Yuan; shared tools: PHC, ProtoMotions, MimicKit, Isaac Lab). Robotics adds domain randomisation and hardware limits; animation adds stylisation and richer conditioning.
- The "prompt by reward / goal / clip" idea of Motivo-type models is the closest learned analogue of what a procedural library exposes as an API (go here, reach there, hit this). It currently costs tens of millions of parameters and mocap, and reaches only about 60-80% of specialist performance.

### Gaps
- GR00T N1/N1.5/N1.6 model details were not read directly. HumanPlus not re-read.
- No source was found of a behavioural foundation model for non-humanoid or 2D bodies.

## 5. Athletic and object-directed skills: how are outcome targets (ball speed, direction) specified and achieved?

### Takeaway
Outcome targets are almost always given to the policy as a goal vector in the character's local frame (a target ball velocity, a target landing point, a target position), and enforced by a reward on the measured or ballistically *predicted* outcome shortly after contact, on top of a mocap-derived motion prior that keeps the movement human-like. Accuracy is good but not exact: the best 2025 football kicker hits target velocity within about 5 degrees and about 4.5 m/s.

### Cited Findings
- [foundation] DeepMimic already combined an imitation reward with a task reward such as throwing a ball at a user-specified target. — [DeepMimic project page](https://xbpeng.github.io/projects/DeepMimic/index.html)
- [foundation] DeepMind humanoid football ("From Motor Control to Team Play in Simulated Humanoid Football", arXiv 2021 / Science Robotics 2022): three stages — low-level human-like motor control via imitation, mid-level skills such as dribbling and shooting, then multi-agent team play with population-based training. — [arXiv 2105.12196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.12196)
- [foundation] Tennis (Zhang, Yuan, Makoviychuk, Guo, Fidler, Peng, Fatahalian; ACM TOG 42(4), SIGGRAPH 2023): hierarchical — a low-level imitation policy and a high-level planning policy acting through a motion embedding learned from broadcast video; video-estimated motion is corrected by physics-based imitation; a hybrid control policy overrides erroneous parts of the embedding (notably the racket arm); characters hit incoming balls to target positions with topspin and slice and sustain rallies; the ball-position reward depends on the estimated outgoing bounce position; needs only simple rewards and no stroke-type labels. — [NVIDIA project page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/); [NVIDIA publication page](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2023-08_learning-physically-simulated-tennis-players-broadcast-videos)
- [foundation] SMPLOlympics / HumanoidOlympics (2024): Isaac Gym sports suite (high jump, long jump, hurdling, golf, javelin, table tennis, tennis, fencing, boxing, soccer, basketball). Target specification examples: soccer penalty kick — ball 12 m from goal, random target inside the posts, rewards for approaching the ball, ball velocity toward the goal and *predicted landing point*; basketball free throw from 4.5 m — reward for matching a *physics-derived desired ball velocity*; tennis/table tennis — racket-to-ball proximity plus predicted landing spot near target; golf — predicted landing-point reward; boxing/fencing — contact force above a threshold on target body parts. — [SMPLOlympics arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00187)
- SMPLOlympics results: only methods using the PULSE motion prior solved golf, penalty kick and free throw consistently; plain PPO scored 0% on golf and penalty kick (91.4% on free throw); AMP scored 0% on penalty kick and free throw because its style reward overrode the task. High jump and hurdling failed without a curriculum. Training: 1-3 days on one RTX 3090, about 10^9 samples, MLP (2048, 1024, 512). — [SMPLOlympics arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00187)
- [2025-26] PhysicsFC (Kim, Jung, Lee; SIGGRAPH 2025 / ACM TOG): user-controlled physics-based football player with dribble, trap, move and kick skill policies that output latents for a pretrained CALM motion embedding, tied together by a finite state machine and "skill transition-based initialisation" (start each skill's training episodes from states produced by the preceding skill). — [PhysicsFC arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21216)
- PhysicsFC kick specifics: the goal input is a 3D target ball velocity in the character's frame; training samples horizontal direction within ±45° of forward, elevation 0-45°, speed 5-35 m/s. The reward is an exponential of the squared, speed-normalised difference between target and actual ball velocity, evaluated only for 1/3 s after contact; 70% of episodes start from dribbling states. Result over 1,000 random targets (standing start, ball 1 m ahead): 99.9% contact success, mean direction error 4.79°, mean speed error 4.51 m/s; a box-shaped foot collider is worse (6.62°, 6.35 m/s). — [PhysicsFC HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21216)
- PhysicsFC cost: Isaac Gym, 60 Hz sim / 30 Hz policy, single NVIDIA A6000; kick policy 4,096 envs, 30 hours, 5.5 billion samples; the low-level policy took 30 days and 5 billion samples; motion data is only about three minutes (90 clips). Dribbling under-tracks target speed above 5 m/s; trap has a 5.6% handball rate. — [PhysicsFC HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21216)
- [2025-26] SkillMimic (CVPR 2025): one policy learns basketball skills (dribble, shoot, retrieve) from human-object interaction demonstrations with 84.7%-94.5% per-skill success (from search summary). — [SkillMimic CVPR paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Wang_SkillMimic_Learning_Basketball_Interaction_Skills_from_Demonstrations_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)
- CLoSD specifies strike targets through text plus a target location ("strike with a hand or foot"). — [CLoSD arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03441)
- SONIC's kinematic planner exposes boxing as a selectable style rather than an outcome target. — [SONIC paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07820v3)

### Inferences
- The standard pattern for "kick the ball at speed v in direction d" is: goal = desired ball velocity vector (character frame); reward = closeness of actual post-contact ball velocity, measured in a short window after contact, or closeness of a ballistically predicted landing point. This pattern is independent of learning: a hand-written controller can use the same specification (invert the contact/ballistics to a required foot velocity at impact) and the same evaluation metric (direction error in degrees, speed error in m/s).
- PhysicsFC's errors (about 5°, about 4.5 m/s over a 5-35 m/s range) are a useful realism benchmark: even SOTA learned kickers are approximate, and foot collision geometry measurably matters (rounded foot beats box).
- The FSM-plus-transition-aware-initialisation structure is directly portable: hand-written skills also fail mostly at hand-offs, and testing each skill from the end-states of its predecessors is the non-learned analogue.
- Unconstrained RL with only task reward gives unnatural or failed motion; some motion prior (mocap latent, or for a procedural system, a hand-authored pose/timing template) is what makes outcome-driven skills look right.

### Gaps
- Tennis paper's quantitative hit rate / landing error and exactly how target and spin are fed to the policy were not retrieved (project pages are qualitative).
- DeepMind football compute (simulator, years of experience) not retrieved from the abstract page.
- No 2025-2026 source on learned throwing accuracy specifically (beyond javelin distance in SMPLOlympics) was found; PhysicsFC limitations section was truncated.
- SkillMimic details come from a search summary only.

## 6. Practical cost: training time, GPUs, simulators, and evidence of small-scale or browser deployment

### Takeaway
Training is GPU-bound and large (single-GPU days for one skill; a GPU-week for universal trackers; 128 GPUs for a week at the top end), but *inference* is cheap — the deployed policies are small MLPs or GRUs that run in 1-2 ms — and by 2026 there is an established pattern of running exported ONNX policies with MuJoCo-WASM physics entirely in the browser.

### Cited Findings
**Training cost ladder**
- Single skill with a pretrained prior: 30 hours, 4,096 envs, 5.5 billion samples on one A6000 (PhysicsFC kick); the prior itself: 30 days on one A6000. — [PhysicsFC HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21216)
- Sports tasks: 1-3 days on one RTX 3090, about 10^9 samples. — [SMPLOlympics arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00187)
- Universal tracker: about one week on one A100, about 10^10 samples (PHC). — [PHC arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.06456)
- Diffusion controllers: 8 RTX 4090s for about 12 h (MIND); about 10 GPU-days on one GPU (UniPhys); 8 V100s for about 70 h (PDP tracking). — [MIND](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26006); [UniPhys](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12540); [PDP](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.00960)
- Top end: 128 GPUs for 7 days = 21k GPU-hours (SONIC). — [SONIC paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07820v3)
- Kinematic text-to-motion at scale: 16 A100-80GB GPUs, two phases of 500k steps (Kimodo). — [Kimodo arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15546)
- Low end [2025-26]: nanoG1 trains a 29-DoF Unitree G1 walking policy from scratch (no mocap) in 58.9 seconds on one RTX PRO 6000 — 75M samples at 1.28M steps/s, about $0.17 — using a G1-specialised CUDA physics engine validated against MuJoCo; the first version took 6.1 hours. A left-right symmetry loss was the biggest single factor in getting under 60 s. (Community project on Hugging Face, not peer-reviewed.) — [nanoG1 model card](https://huggingface.co/kingJulio/nanoG1)

**Simulators**
- Isaac Gym remains the default in animation papers through 2025-26 (PHC, PULSE, SMPLOlympics, PhysicsFC, UniPhys, MIND, MimicKit experiments). — [MimicKit](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13794v4); [MIND](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26006)
- Isaac Lab is used for SONIC and BFM-Zero; MuJoCo for Meta Motivo. — [SONIC](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07820v3); [Meta Motivo](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11054v1)
- [2025-26] Newton: open-source GPU-accelerated differentiable physics engine from NVIDIA, Google DeepMind and Disney Research, built on NVIDIA Warp and OpenUSD, integrating MuJoCo Warp as a backend; announced 2025, managed via the Linux Foundation; a "Newton 1.0 GA" at GTC 2026 is reported by the search summary of NVIDIA's blog (vendor source). — [NVIDIA: announcing Newton](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/announcing-newton-an-open-source-physics-engine-for-robotics-simulation/); [Hugging Face/NVIDIA: State of Simulation for Physical AI](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/state-of-simulation-for-physical-ai)
- [2025-26] mjlab: lightweight framework pairing Isaac Lab's manager-based API with MuJoCo Warp; ships a Unitree G1 velocity-tracking example; needs an NVIDIA GPU for training. — [mjlab GitHub](https://github.com/mujocolab/mjlab)
- Throughput figures (single-source, community): nanoG1's custom engine 7.25M steps/s vs 4.0M for mujoco_warp, 2.3M for Genesis, 1.1M for MJX on identical G1 settings. — [nanoG1 model card](https://huggingface.co/kingJulio/nanoG1)
- ProtoMotions3 lists Newton (beta), Isaac Gym, Isaac Lab, Genesis, MuJoCo backends; MimicKit lists Isaac Gym, Isaac Lab, Newton. — [ProtoMotions docs](https://nvlabs.github.io/ProtoMotions); [MimicKit](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13794v4)

**Policy size and inference**
- nanoG1 policy: MinGRU recurrent net, 3 layers of 128 units, 163.9K parameters, 98-D observation, 29-D action (PD joint targets). — [nanoG1 model card](https://huggingface.co/kingJulio/nanoG1)
- PHC/SMPLOlympics policies are plain MLPs ([1024, 512] or [2048, 1024, 512]). — [PHC](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.06456); [SMPLOlympics](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00187)
- SONIC: 1.2M-42M parameters; 1-2 ms per forward pass on Jetson Orin at 50 Hz. — [SONIC](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07820v3)
- Diffusion controllers are the exception: about 10-18 FPS (UniPhys); seconds per clip for Kimodo. — [UniPhys](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12540); [Kimodo](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15546)

**Browser / WASM / ONNX deployment [2025-26]**
- mjswan (v0.12.12, uploaded 2026-11-10, Apache-2.0, alpha): Python package (plus npm package) that builds static-site demos running MuJoCo WASM + ONNX Runtime + three.js entirely in the browser with real-time RL policy control, no server; ports most mjlab tasks; live demos include MuscleMimic and GentleHumanoid. — [mjswan on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/mjswan/)
- muwanx is a related/predecessor package with the same stack (from search summary). — [muwanx docs](https://muwanx.readthedocs.io/)
- nanoG1 has a live demo where both physics and policy run in the browser, driven by arrow keys, with a self-contained CPU/WASM example needing no MuJoCo or CUDA at inference. — [nanoG1 model card](https://huggingface.co/kingJulio/nanoG1)
- A community three.js post describes a robot-arm policy running fully in-browser with MuJoCo WASM and onnxruntime-web, trying WebGPU + fp16 first with multi-threaded WASM fallback (from search summary). — [three.js forum post](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/a-real-robot-policy-neural-net-physics-running-100-in-the-browser-so-101-arm-three-js-r3f/92415)
- Other in-browser policy demos surfaced by search (not opened): a Go1 quantisation benchmark running MuJoCo WASM, MicroDuck biped ONNX policies with a web viewer, MuJoCo WASM Play. — [quant-control-bench-policies](https://huggingface.co/happynood/quant-control-bench-policies); [MicroDuck](https://huggingface.co/kyoungsim/microduck); [MuJoCo WASM Play docs](https://mujoco-wasm-play.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)

### Inferences
- The cost asymmetry is the key practical fact: training needs Python, CUDA and a GPU simulator; inference of a locomotion-class policy is a few hundred thousand multiply-adds per step. A 3x128 MLP forward pass is trivially implementable in dependency-free TypeScript (no ONNX runtime needed) and would run in microseconds.
- The hard requirement for borrowing a learned policy is simulator parity: the browser demos work because they run the *same* physics (MuJoCo WASM) as training. A custom 2D TypeScript physics engine has no GPU-parallel training twin, so training would have to run in that engine itself (e.g. headless in Node, CPU-parallel) or the policy would face a sim-to-sim gap.
- nanoG1 shows that with a specialised fast simulator and tricks like symmetry losses, from-scratch locomotion RL without mocap has become very cheap in wall-clock terms — but the resulting gait quality is robot-like, not stylised animation, and the result is from a community model card.
- Symmetry (mirroring left/right) is a cheap prior worth borrowing in any controller design or tuning loop.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed benchmark comparing Isaac Lab / Newton / MJX / Genesis / Brax throughput in 2026 was found; figures above are vendor or community reported. Brax was not covered by any retrieved source.
- An "NVIDIA Metatron v1" browser humanoid appeared in a search summary but was not opened or verified; treat as unconfirmed.
- No evidence found of WebGPU being used for *training* character policies in the browser at meaningful scale.
- ProtoMotions "AMASS in 12 hours on 4 A100s" is unverified (secondary page).

## 7. Learned work on 2D / planar / very low-DOF characters and tiny policies that could run in JavaScript

### Takeaway
There is essentially no current (2025-2026) research targeting 2D stick-figure or planar character animation with learned controllers; planar bipeds survive as RL benchmarks (Walker2d) and hobby projects, and the only directly comparable artefact found is an old browser 2D ragdoll walker trained with DDPG that needed 8-44 GPU-hours to walk "ish".

### Cited Findings
- wassname/rl_2d_walker.js: a 2D humanoid ragdoll walker (walker code adapted from rednuht.org genetic walkers; physics appears to be Box2D) trained offline in Node with TensorFlow.js DDPG and displayed in the browser; README says a CUDA GPU with 8GB+ is ideal and training "will take 8 hours to see results, 44 hours for good results"; self-described goal is "teaching a humanoid to walk(ish)"; 10 stars, no stated update date. — [rl_2d_walker.js GitHub](https://www.github.com/wassname/rl_2d_walker.js)
- Walker2d (Gymnasium/MuJoCo) is the standard planar biped benchmark: 6 actuated joints, 17-D observation, torque actions (not re-verified). — [Gymnasium Walker2d docs](https://gymnasium.farama.org/environments/mujoco/walker2d/)
- MotrixLab documents a "2D Walker Robot" demo based on the DeepMind Control walker (seen in search results only). — [MotrixLab dm_walker](https://motrixlab.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user_guide/demo/dm_walker.html)
- Xue Bin Peng's early (pre-DeepMimic) MSc work, "Developing Locomotion Skills with Deep Reinforcement Learning", used planar simulated characters (not re-verified beyond the title appearing in search results). — [Peng MSc thesis page](https://xbpeng.github.io/projects/MSc_Thesis/index.html)
- Mimic2DM (arXiv Dec 2025), "Learning to Control Physically-simulated 3D Characters via Generating and Mimicking 2D Motions", uses 2D *motion data* (e.g. from video) to train 3D physics characters — it is not a planar-character method. — [Mimic2DM arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08500v1)
- A targeted search for a 2D Box2D / TensorFlow.js DeepMimic-style stick-figure imitation project returned nothing matching. — search performed 2026-11-12; closest hits were [DeepMimic](https://xbpeng.github.io/projects/DeepMimic) and [Mimic2DM](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08500v1)
- Smallest credible modern whole-body policy found: 163.9K-parameter MinGRU for a 29-DoF 3D humanoid, runnable in browser WASM. — [nanoG1 model card](https://huggingface.co/kingJulio/nanoG1)
- A 2026 arXiv paper, "Learning Smooth Time-Varying Linear Policies with an Action Jacobian Penalty" (2602.18312), concerns very simple (linear, time-varying) policy classes for physics-based control (title-level evidence from search; content not read). — [arXiv 2602.18312](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18312)

### Inferences
- The 2D physics stick-figure niche is unserved by current research; a hand-written controller library is not competing with a known learned 2D system. The SOTA is entirely 3D humanoid and its artefacts (SMPL bodies, AMASS mocap, Isaac Gym) do not map onto a 2D stick figure.
- A planar figure has roughly 6-12 actuated DOF versus 23-69 for the 3D humanoids above, so if learning were attempted, networks of a few thousand to a few tens of thousands of parameters are plausible by analogy with Walker2d-class tasks and with nanoG1's 164K for 29 DoF; this is an extrapolation, not a cited result.
- Realistic borrowings at small scale, ranked by how little infrastructure they need:
  1. Structural ideas with zero learning: low-level balance/tracking layer + high-level intent layer (PHC/CLoSD split); phase variables for cyclic motion (PFNN/DeepPhase); FSM with transition-aware testing (PhysicsFC); goal = desired ball velocity vector with post-contact evaluation window (PhysicsFC/SMPLOlympics); fall-recovery as its own primitive (PHC); left/right symmetry.
  2. Offline optimisation of hand-written controller *parameters* against rollouts in the library's own simulator (the same outcome metrics as the RL rewards), which needs no neural network at runtime.
  3. A tiny residual or cloned MLP trained offline in Node against the library's own physics, using PDP-style noisy-state/clean-action data from the existing hand-written controller, shipped as plain weight arrays. Feasible in principle; no published precedent was found for 2D stick figures.
  4. Full RL imitation (DeepMimic/AMP-style) in 2D: would need 2D reference motion, a fast headless build of the engine, and substantial tuning; the only precedent found took 8-44 GPU-hours for mediocre walking.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed 2025-2026 work on learned control of 2D stick figures or 2D ragdoll game characters was found; absence is based on two targeted searches, not an exhaustive review.
- No measured numbers were found for minimal network sizes that solve planar biped tasks, nor for pure-JavaScript (non-ONNX, non-WASM) policy inference performance.
- Evolutionary / genetic 2D walker demos (e.g. the rednuht.org walkers that rl_2d_walker.js derives from) were not investigated.
