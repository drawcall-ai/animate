# Classical (non-learned) physics-based and procedural character animation control — foundations to November 2026

Reader notes on provenance:
- "Read in full text" means I extracted the PDF and read the passages quoted. "Abstract only" or "search snippet" means I did not open the full text; treat numbers from those with more caution.
- Dates in brackets after each technique mark it as HISTORICAL (superseded in research practice but still implementable and useful), CURRENT (actively used or published 2024–2026), or BOTH.
- Notation: x = horizontal CoM position, v = ẋ, h = CoM height, g = gravity, Tc = sqrt(h/g), ω = 1/Tc.

## 1. Balance and foot placement: SIMBICON, Generalized Biped Walking Control, LIPM, capture point / DCM, Raibert

### Takeaway
Every classical stepping rule reduces to "place the swing foot at a linear function of CoM offset and CoM velocity": x_foot = a + b·v with b near sqrt(h/g) (capture point), and the linear inverted pendulum gives closed-form stability bounds on b that widen as step time shrinks. SIMBICON (2007) and Coros et al. (2010) are the two graphics-side recipes with published gains and push-robustness numbers; both are historical in research terms but remain the simplest things that work on a planar biped, and both were run at fixed small timesteps (0.1 ms and 0.5 ms).

### Cited Findings

**SIMBICON (Yin, Loken, van de Panne, SIGGRAPH 2007) [HISTORICAL, still the standard baseline] — read in full text**
- Structure: a finite state machine where each state holds a target pose of joint angles relative to parent links; walking needs as few as four states, running as few as two; transitions fire after an elapsed time or on foot contact, and a state whose contact condition is already met is skipped — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Per-joint actuation is plain PD: τ = kp(θd − θ) − kd·θ̇; target poses "are typically not actually achieved" (they are attractors, not keyframes) — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Torso and swing hip are controlled in the world frame by virtual PD controllers (τ_torso, τ_B); the stance hip torque is the free variable that makes this physically realisable: τ_A = −τ_torso − τ_B — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Balance feedback law, applied to the swing hip target: θd = θd0 + cd·d + cv·v, where d is horizontal distance from stance ankle to CoM and v is CoM velocity; the hip midpoint is "a simple and effective proxy" for the CoM in both 2D and 3D. General form: θd = θd0 + F·[d v]ᵀ with F an n×2 matrix (used e.g. for stance-ankle feedback in quiet standing) — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Why both d and v: cd matters for low-speed and in-place stepping; (d, v) together identify the phase of the step, whereas v alone only gives velocity error — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- 2D model parameters: 6 internal DOF (9 total); kp = 300 Nm/rad, kd = 30 Nms/rad for all joints; penalty ground contact with kp = 100000 N/m, kd = 6000 Ns/m; friction 0.65; timestep 0.0001 s; torque limit 1000 Nm (370 Nm suffices except fast run; basic walk survives down to 90 Nm, below which it is "weak-kneed and falls"); 5x real time on a 1.8 GHz CoreDuo — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- 3D model: ODE 0.6, timestep 0.005 s, kd = 0.1·kp, kp ≤ 300 except waist (1000) — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Gains: cd, cv "usually within [0, 1]"; basic 3D walk uses cd = 0.5, cv = 0.2. Stable ranges found by sweeping one parameter: cd ∈ [−0.71, 1.4], cv ∈ [0.03, 0.59] (sagittal); cd ∈ [−1.29, 1.13], cv ∈ [0.06, 0.48] (coronal) — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Documented failure modes: cv too low → velocity accumulates until the character falls; cv too high → rocking with growing amplitude; near the upper limits, period doubling and chaotic behaviour appear — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Robustness (2D/3D): unanticipated 20 cm down-steps and ±6° slopes; walking withstands 0.1 s pushes of 600 N forward / 500 N backward at all 10 sampled gait phases; skipping only 40 N / 50 N. Steeper slopes need an added target-angle displacement — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Stated limitations: feedback gains are still hand-tuned; mocap pipeline only tested on walking; gaits not energy-optimised; no reaction-time delay; stability shown only by simulation experiments, not analysis — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Positioning versus Raibert: SIMBICON adapts continuously during the step and uses position and velocity, whereas Raibert/Hodgins-style control used velocity only — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)

**Generalized Biped Walking Control (Coros, Beaudoin, van de Panne, SIGGRAPH 2010, TOG 29(4) art. 130) [HISTORICAL, most complete classical recipe] — read in full text**
- Four components: (1) motion generator producing target trajectories tracked by PD; (2) Jacobian-transpose gravity compensation; (3) inverted-pendulum foot placement; (4) a Jacobian-transpose virtual force on the CoM for fine velocity control. Claimed to need "no character-specific or motion-specific tuning" — [GBWC project page](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/index.html), [GBWC paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Foot placement derivation: constant-leg-length inverted pendulum, equate energy now with energy at balanced rest: ½mv² + mgh = mg·sqrt(h² + d²). Solving for the step distance d that brings velocity to zero: d = v·sqrt(h/g + v²/(4g²)). (The square root is lost in PDF text extraction; this form follows from the stated energy balance.) — [GBWC paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Desired speed enters as an offset: d′ = d − α·Vd, α = 0.05 for all results (α = 0.2 when pushing/pulling a crate); a shorter step yields positive residual velocity, a longer one negative. α = 0 works for |Vd| ≤ 0.4 m/s; doubling α did not hurt robustness, but large α makes the predicted foot location unsuitable for balance — [GBWC paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Target (xd, zd) is recomputed every timestep using the true CoM; applied independently in sagittal and coronal planes — [GBWC paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Velocity tuning: FV = kV(Vd − V) with kV = 100 Ns/m, mapped to stance-leg torques by τV = J_Vᵀ·FV — [GBWC paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Gravity compensation: virtual force Fi = −mi·g at each link CoM, τ = Σ Jiᵀ Fi; removing it gives slouched posture and inaccurate swing-foot placement; it is "key to achieving successful motions without resorting to excessively high PD gains" — [GBWC paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Parameters: ODE, timestep 0.0005 s; kp scaled by character mass ratio M′/M (without scaling, large characters' knees buckle); friction 0.8; no torque limits in shown results — [GBWC paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Robustness: 70.4 kg character walking at 0.6 m/s survives omnidirectional 600 N, 0.1 s pushes at arbitrary gait phase; standing push recovery works by invoking walking with Vd = 0 when the CoM leaves a safe region — [GBWC paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Limitations stated: push-recovery style cannot be authored (it is dictated by the pendulum model); swing/stance leg self-intersection; no terrain generalisation except stairs; no high-speed agility — [GBWC paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Source code was released as cartwheel-3d — [GBWC project page](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/index.html)

**Linear inverted pendulum / ZMP [HISTORICAL foundation, CURRENT as a reduced model]**
- Planar moment balance about the centre of pressure: (m·z̈ + mg)(x_cp − x) = (z_cp − z)·m·ẍ − I·θ̈. With constant height and no angular acceleration this becomes ẍ = g·(x − x_cp)/(z − z_cp) — the LIPM/ZMP equation — [Tedrake, Underactuated Robotics ch. 5](https://underactuated.mit.edu/humanoids.html)
- Constraints: the CoP must lie inside the convex hull of contact points; unilateral contact gives z̈ ≥ −g; Coulomb friction gives |ẍ| ≤ μ(z̈ + g) — [Tedrake ch. 5](https://underactuated.mit.edu/humanoids.html)
- Closed-form LIPM solution over a step of duration T with a point foot at the origin: x(T) = x0·cosh(T/Tc) + Tc·v0·sinh(T/Tc); v(T) = x0·sinh(T/Tc)/Tc + v0·cosh(T/Tc), Tc = sqrt(h/g) — [Ye et al., "The Simplest Balance Controller for Dynamic Walking", arXiv 2211.06223](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.06223)
- Recommended strategy from the centroidal view: hold ż constant and minimise angular momentum about the CoM; a swing foot landing roughly under the CoM then dissipates little energy — [Tedrake ch. 5](https://underactuated.mit.edu/humanoids.html)

**Capture point / instantaneous capture point (Pratt et al. 2006; Koolen et al. 2012) [BOTH]**
- Definition under LIPM: cp = x + sqrt(h/g)·ẋ; placing and holding the CoP there brings the CoM to rest above it — [Kim et al., arXiv 1901.08100, eq. 45, citing Koolen 2012](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.08100)
- Original references: Pratt, Carff, Drakunov, Goswami, "Capture point: A step toward humanoid push recovery", Humanoids 2006, pp. 200–207; Koolen et al., "Capturability-based analysis and control of legged locomotion, Part 1", IJRR 31(9):1094–1113, 2012 — [citation list in NSF-hosted paper](https://par.nsf.gov//servlets/purl/10312892)
- Generalisation to a vertically accelerating support: x_cap = sqrt(z0/(z̈_ws + g))·ẋ_sc, with ẋ_sc the CoM velocity relative to the support point — [NSF-hosted paper](https://par.nsf.gov//servlets/purl/10312892)
- ICP dynamics: the instantaneous capture point moves along the line through the point foot and itself, away from the foot, at a speed proportional to its distance from the foot (first-order unstable) — [summary of Koolen et al. 2012](https://humon-research-lab.github.io/freemocap_slackline_project/lit_review/dynamic_balance_and_locomotion_papers/N-Step%20Capturability%20-%20Koolen%20Et%20Al.%202012)

**Linear foot placement control: explicit stability bounds (Ye, Wang, Liu, Liang, arXiv Nov 2022) [CURRENT, analysis of the classical rule] — read in full text**
- Rule: at each step, place the foot at x_f = a + b·v ahead of the CoM, where v is CoM velocity at step time, step period T fixed — [arXiv 2211.06223](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.06223)
- With sT = sinh(T/Tc), cT = cosh(T/Tc), the step-to-step velocity eigenvalue is λ = cT − b·sT/Tc; walking is stable for b between b_min = Tc(cT − 1)/sT (λ = +1, velocity never decays) and b_max = Tc(cT + 1)/sT (λ = −1, velocity flips sign each step) — [arXiv 2211.06223](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.06223)
- Dead-beat gain: b_db = Tc·cT/sT = Tc·coth(T/Tc) gives λ = 0, i.e. the robot stops (or reaches steady state) in exactly one step — [arXiv 2211.06223](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.06223)
- Capture-point gain: b_cp = Tc is the only constant gain, independent of T, that is stable for every step period; it lies in the overdamped region (stops gradually, no oscillation) — [arXiv 2211.06223](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.06223)
- Robustness metric: the stable interval (b_min, b_max) widens as T shrinks — "faster stepping, easier to balance"; the constant a sets steady-state speed/gait — [arXiv 2211.06223](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.06223)
- Validation was in Matlab and V-REP simulation, 2D and 3D; no hardware — [arXiv 2211.06223](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.06223)

**Raibert hopping foot placement (Raibert et al. 1984/1986) [HISTORICAL, still used as a heuristic]**
- Rule: p = (T_st/2)·ẋ + K(ẋ − ẋd), T_st = stance duration; the first term approximates the "neutral point" (foot position giving a symmetric stance and zero net acceleration), the second displaces the foot to accelerate/decelerate — [Kim et al., arXiv 1901.08100, eq. 43](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.08100)
- Raibert, capture point and the paper's own planner are all instances of p = kp·x + kd·ẋ; capture point is kp = 1, kd = sqrt(h/g) — [arXiv 1901.08100](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.08100)
- Time-to-velocity-reversal planner used on real passive-ankle bipeds (Mercury, Cassie-class): gain K = (1 + κ)·ω⁻¹·coth(ω·t′), with t′ ≈ 0.2 s and κ ≈ 0.16–0.25; it places the foot so that CoM velocity reverses before reaching the foot, rather than stopping on it — [arXiv 1901.08100](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.08100)
- Placing the foot ahead of/behind the neutral point changes speed; later analysis of the discrete hopper dynamics found period-2 and higher-period behaviour even when period-1 hopping is intended — search-snippet level only, from [arXiv 1805.05980](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.05980) and related results

**Divergent component of motion (Englsberger, Ott, Albu-Schäffer, IROS 2013; IEEE T-RO 2015) [CURRENT in robotics]**
- 3D DCM: ξ = x + b·ẋ with time constant b (b = sqrt(Δz/g) = 1/ω); it is the 3D generalisation of the capture point — [Englsberger et al. (CORE copy)](https://core.ac.uk/download/31011906.pdf)
- Tracking law: r_vrp,c = ξ + kξ·b·(ξ − ξd) − b·ξ̇d, yielding a stable closed loop in (x, ξ): the CoM follows the DCM passively, only the DCM needs control — [Englsberger et al. (CORE copy)](https://core.ac.uk/download/31011906.pdf)
- Bibliographic: IROS 2013 DOI 10.1109/IROS.2013.6696723; T-RO 2015 DOI 10.1109/TRO.2015.2405592 — [DLR elib record](https://elib.dlr.de/96741/)

### Inferences
- For the author's 2D figure, the minimal robust stepping rule is: at swing start (and continuously during swing), target the foot at x_foot = x_com + b·v_com − (offset for desired speed), with b ∈ [Tc, Tc·coth(T/Tc)]. b = Tc is the safe default because it is stable for any step duration; b_db is the fastest recovery if step time is known. For h = 1 m, Tc ≈ 0.32 s, so a 1 m/s velocity error asks for a ~0.32 m step.
- The LFPC bounds give a principled replacement for hand-tuned constants: gains become functions of h, g and step time rather than magic numbers, and a "can I recover?" test falls out (if the required step exceeds leg reach, one step is insufficient — take a faster or second step, or fall deliberately).
- SIMBICON's cd/cv are a joint-angle-space version of the same rule (Δθ_hip ≈ step offset / leg length), which explains its stable ranges; converting to a Cartesian foot target plus IK (as GBWC does) removes the dependence on leg length and is why GBWC needed no per-character tuning.
- Both SIMBICON and GBWC report results at one fixed small timestep (0.1 ms / 0.5 ms) with explicit PD and (for SIMBICON 2D) penalty contact. Neither claims timestep independence; their brittleness to timestep is the same class of problem the author sees at 1/1440 s.
- Scripted foot placement fails outside tuned scenarios precisely because it lacks the b·v term; adding continuous CoM-state feedback to the scripted target is the smallest change with the largest robustness payoff.
- All LIPM-derived rules assume constant CoM height, zero angular momentum and instantaneous, impact-free stepping; on a point-mass/rod figure with finite swing time, expect to need the swing-time-aware gain (coth form) or a small empirical offset like GBWC's α.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the full text of Pratt et al. 2006 or Koolen et al. 2012; capture-point formulas are cited from papers that restate them. The N-step capturability region formulas (how far the ICP may be from the foot for 1, 2, ... step recovery, with finite foot size and reaction wheel) were not verified and are omitted.
- The GBWC paper states "kd = 2·kp" in extracted text; a square root may have been lost in extraction (2·sqrt(kp) would be critical damping for unit inertia). Check the PDF before quoting.
- No source read for ankle/hip strategies (CoP shifting within the foot, angular-momentum "lunge") as explicit formulas; the author's stick figure may have point feet, in which case only stepping and angular momentum are available.
- The exact DCM dynamics equation ξ̇ = ω(ξ − r_vrp) was not seen verbatim in a primary source.
- No quantitative comparison found of SIMBICON vs capture-point stepping on the same planar model.

## 2. Joint actuation: PD, Stable PD, implicit damping, torque limits, virtual model control

### Takeaway
Explicit PD is conditionally stable (the stable timestep shrinks as gains rise), which is the textbook cause of "only works at 1/1440 s"; Stable PD (Tan, Liu, Turk 2011) fixes this by evaluating the PD law at the next timestep, which in 1-DOF form is a one-line change: divide by (m + kd·Δt) instead of m. Modern engines (MuJoCo's implicitfast) bake the same idea into the integrator, and gravity compensation / Jacobian-transpose virtual forces let gains stay low in the first place.

### Cited Findings

**Conventional PD and why it is timestep-dependent [HISTORICAL baseline]**
- Conventional discrete PD: τⁿ = −kp(qⁿ − q̄ⁿ) − kd·q̇ⁿ; with high kp the control force becomes numerically unstable unless the timestep is reduced significantly, so "PD controllers suffer from" a coupling between high gain and small timestep — [Tan, Liu, Turk, "Stable Proportional-Derivative Controllers", IEEE CG&A 2011](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf)
- SIMBICON-era practice was low gains and tiny steps: kp = 300, kd = 30 at Δt = 0.0001 s (2D) — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- High-gain tracking yields "stiff and reactive motion"; SIMBICON's remedy was feedback-error learning of feed-forward torques so that gains could be lowered — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)

**Stable PD (SPD) (Tan, Liu, Turk; IEEE CG&A 2011, DOI 10.1109/MCG.2011.30) [CURRENT — default in most physics-based character work] — read in full text**
- Idea: compute the PD force from the state at the next step: τⁿ = −kp(qⁿ⁺¹ − q̄ⁿ⁺¹) − kd·q̇ⁿ⁺¹, with first-order Taylor expansion qⁿ⁺¹ = qⁿ + Δt·q̇ⁿ, q̇ⁿ⁺¹ = q̇ⁿ + Δt·q̈ⁿ, giving τⁿ = −kp(qⁿ + Δt·q̇ⁿ − q̄ⁿ⁺¹) − kd(q̇ⁿ + Δt·q̈ⁿ) — [SPD paper](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf)
- With velocity tracking: τⁿ = −kp(qⁿ + Δt·q̇ⁿ − q̄ⁿ⁺¹) − kd(q̇ⁿ + Δt·q̈ⁿ − q̄̇ⁿ⁺¹) — [SPD paper](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf)
- 1-DOF closed form (mass m, position p): q̈ = −[kp(p + Δt·ṗ − q̄) + kd·ṗ] / (m + kd·Δt) — [SPD paper](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf)
- Multi-DOF: q̈ⁿ = (M + Kd·Δt)⁻¹·(−C − Kp(qⁿ + q̇ⁿΔt − q̄ⁿ⁺¹) − Kd·q̇ⁿ + τ_ext), then integrate with explicit Euler; Kp, Kd diagonal — [SPD paper](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf)
- Stability condition: stable provided Kd ≥ Kp·Δt (appendix analysis); the paper's extreme example sets kd = kp·Δt with infinite gains and still converges in two Euler steps — [SPD paper](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf)
- Versus a fully implicit integrator: implicit is unconditionally stable but needs linearised force derivatives and "usually overly damps the motion", which hurts timing-critical motions (their example: catching a basketball); SPD needs no linearisation and does not over-damp — [SPD paper](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf)
- Experiment: two-link pendulum at Δt = 1/60 s. kp = 30 or 300: both PD and SPD stable, SPD less oscillatory. kp = 3000 or 30000: SPD tracks closely, PD "fails to maintain stability and eventually crashes the simulation". SPD's maximum step was bounded by the 30 fps reference sampling, not by stability — [SPD paper](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf)
- Claimed headline: "allows arbitrarily high gains, even at large time steps", demonstrated at 33 ms steps with explicit Euler — [SPD paper](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf)
- Cost: original SPD implementations use an O(n³) dense factorisation of (M + Kd·Δt); Yin & Yin, "Linear Time Stable PD Controllers for Physics-based Character Animation" (Computer Graphics Forum / SCA 2020, DOI 10.1111/cgf.14113) give an O(n) algorithm — [Eurographics DL](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14113)

**Implicit damping in engines [CURRENT]**
- MuJoCo does not support explicit Euler at all; its "Euler" is semi-implicit and treats only joint damping implicitly — [MuJoCo computation docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html)
- implicitfast treats derivatives of all velocity-dependent forces (except constraints and Coriolis terms) implicitly, at "similar computational cost to Euler, yet provides increased stability, and is therefore a strict improvement"; it is the recommended default — [MuJoCo computation docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html)
- Implicit-in-velocity integration is "particularly effective in systems where instabilities are caused by velocity-dependent forces", e.g. "substantial damping in tendons and actuators" — [MuJoCo computation docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html)
- RK4 is better for energy-conserving systems, but "single-step methods can be significantly more stable than RK4" when large velocity-dependent forces are integrated implicitly — [MuJoCo computation docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html)
- MuJoCo position actuators gained a kv (damping) attribute to implement PD with zero reference velocity; the changelog recommends implicit/implicitfast integrators when using it — [MuJoCo changelog](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/blob/1e2e0b3053e8c1866441e154569b6f56a46fc42f/doc/changelog.rst)
- Discretisation bug class worth knowing: Euler-integrated activation filters diverge when the filter time constant is below the timestep, while exactly integrated filters are stable for any positive time constant — [MuJoCo computation docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html)

**Torque limits and gain scaling [BOTH]**
- Torque limits double as a plausibility and stability device: SIMBICON clamps at 1000 Nm (370 Nm typical; walking fails below 90 Nm) — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)
- Gains should scale with mass: GBWC multiplies kp by M′/M when the character is resized; unscaled gains make knees buckle on heavy characters — [GBWC paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Predictive-sampling style planners rely on clamped controls: spline parameters are clamped to control limits so every sample is feasible — [Howell et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.00541)

**Virtual model control / Jacobian transpose (Pratt et al., ICRA 1997; IJRR 20(2):129–143, 2001) [HISTORICAL, concept still current]**
- VMC attaches imaginary springs/dampers to the body and realises their forces with joint torques via the Jacobian transpose (τ = Jᵀ·F); it walked a physical planar biped over level terrain with a simple set of virtual components — [Pratt et al. ICRA 1997 paper](https://www.ftp.ai.mit.edu/people/jpratt/virt_mod.pdf)
- GBWC's use: per-link gravity compensation τ = Σ Jiᵀ(−mi·g) and CoM velocity force τV = J_Vᵀ·kV(Vd − V), applied through the stance leg — [GBWC paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- The author's timestep lock-in almost certainly comes from explicit PD torques (and possibly explicit damping) integrated with the constraint solver. The cheapest fix in a point-mass/rod PBD setting: treat each joint as 1-DOF SPD with an effective inertia — q̈ = −[kp(θ + Δt·θ̇ − θ̄) + kd·θ̇]/(I_eff + kd·Δt) — and choose kd ≥ kp·Δt. I_eff can be the reduced inertia of the two sides about the joint; the diagonal approximation ignores coupling but removes the stiffness instability.
- Expressing gains as natural frequency and damping ratio (kp = I_eff·ωn², kd = 2ζ·I_eff·ωn) rather than raw numbers makes them transfer across masses and timesteps; GBWC's mass scaling is a special case of this.
- In position-based dynamics specifically, an alternative with the same effect is to make the joint drive a compliant angular constraint solved implicitly by the PBD solver; this is the PBD analogue of "implicit PD" — an inference, not something I sourced.
- Gravity compensation (analytic in 2D: for each joint, the torque from the weight of the distal mass about the joint) lets kp drop substantially, which both softens the look and widens the stable timestep range.
- SPD's caveat matters for kicks/throws: fully implicit schemes over-damp timing-critical motion. SPD or semi-implicit damping is preferable to simply cranking up global damping.
- Fixed-timestep with accumulator plus substeps remains necessary for determinism of the "rehearse on a clone" routine regardless of the actuator model; SPD only makes the result much less sensitive to the chosen step.

### Gaps

Its relevance to a 2D rod figure is likely low (torque-angle-velocity limit curves are the transferable part), but that is my judgement, not a sourced claim.
- I did not find a primary source deriving implicit PD inside position-based or XPBD solvers; the PBD-specific advice above is inference.
- The full 2001 IJRR VMC paper was not read; the ICRA 1997 predecessor and bibliographic record were.

## 3. Outcome-targeted actions and optimisation: shooting, collocation, iLQR/DDP, sampling MPC, SAMCON, CIO

### Takeaway
The author's "rehearse on a clone and bisect one parameter" is single shooting with a 1-D root finder; the direct generalisation that is cheap enough for a planar low-DOF figure in a browser is derivative-free sampling over a handful of spline/parameter values (predictive sampling, CEM/MPPI, CMA-ES), minimising a residual vector (speed error, angle error) and reporting the final residual as the feasibility signal. Gradient-based methods (collocation, iLQR) are faster per iteration but need derivatives through contact and cannot themselves distinguish "infeasible" from "stuck in a local minimum".

### Cited Findings

**Formulations (space-time constraints lineage; Tedrake's notes) [BOTH]**
- Direct transcription: decision variables are all states and controls, dynamics are equality constraints x[n+1] = f(x[n], u[n]); (N+1)·dim x + N·dim u variables, banded sparse constraints, well-conditioned, easy state constraints, parallel constraint evaluation — [Tedrake, Underactuated ch. "Trajectory Optimization"](https://underactuated.mit.edu/trajopt.html)
- Direct shooting: only controls are variables, states come from forward simulation; N·dim u variables and unconstrained in the basic case, but poorly conditioned for long horizons ("tail wagging the dog"), simulation is serial, and each state constraint must be unrolled through the dynamics — [Tedrake trajopt](https://underactuated.mit.edu/trajopt.html)
- Direct collocation (Hargraves & Paris 1987): cubic state splines, linear inputs; midpoint x_c = ½(x_k + x_{k+1}) + (h/8)(ẋ_k − ẋ_{k+1}), ẋ_c = −(3/2h)(x_k − x_{k+1}) − ¼(ẋ_k + ẋ_{k+1}); constraint ẋ_c = f(x_c, u_c) per segment; third-order accurate with two dynamics evaluations per segment — [Tedrake trajopt](https://underactuated.mit.edu/trajopt.html)
- Gradients for shooting come from an adjoint (backprop-like) recursion: λ[n−1] = ∂ℓ/∂x[n]ᵀ + ∂f/∂x[n]ᵀ·λ[n]; ∂J/∂u[n] = ∂ℓ/∂u[n] + λ[n]ᵀ·∂f/∂u[n] — [Tedrake trajopt](https://underactuated.mit.edu/trajopt.html)
- iLQR/DDP: linearise dynamics and quadratise cost along the current trajectory, backward Riccati pass gives time-varying feedback gains, forward pass rolls out the nonlinear system; DDP keeps second-order dynamics terms but iLQR usually converges about as fast; constraints only via penalties/augmented Lagrangian — [Tedrake trajopt](https://underactuated.mit.edu/trajopt.html)
- Infeasibility reporting: with local nonconvex solvers, a report of "infeasible" cannot be distinguished from being stuck in a local minimum; remedies are a better initial guess (collocation/transcription accept state guesses, shooting only control guesses) or cost reshaping — [Tedrake trajopt](https://underactuated.mit.edu/trajopt.html)
- Contact: with Coulomb friction the dynamics are a differential inclusion; contact make/break needs special gradient treatment — [Tedrake trajopt](https://underactuated.mit.edu/trajopt.html)
- Early graphics use on athletic motion: Liu & Popović, "Synthesis of Complex Dynamic Character Motion from Simple Animations" (SIGGRAPH 2002) used iterative optimisation with momentum constraints to produce jumping, kicking, running and gymnastics from rough keyframes (offline) — [project page](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/charanim/phylter.html)

**Predictive Sampling and MuJoCo MPC (Howell, Gileadi, Tunyasuvunakool, Zakka, Erez, Tassa; arXiv Dec 2022) [CURRENT] — read in full text**
- MJPC ships three shooting planners: iLQG (second-order), gradient descent, and derivative-free Predictive Sampling — [arXiv 2212.00541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00541)
- Algorithm: controls are a time-indexed spline with parameters θ (cubic Hermite, piecewise-linear or zero-order hold). Each iteration: draw N−1 samples θi ~ N(θ, σ²), clamp to control limits, roll out all N (including the unperturbed nominal) from the current state, set θ ← argmin J(θi). Execute the first action, shift, repeat with warm start — [arXiv 2212.00541](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.00541)
- Only two parameters: number of rollouts N and noise scale σ. It is "MPPI with infinite temperature", "CEM with a non-adaptive distribution" or "trivial random search"; "we are introducing Predictive Sampling not because it is good, but because it is not good" — yet it was "surprisingly competitive" with iLQG — [arXiv 2212.00541](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.00541)
- Why splines: they shrink the search space and bounded spline parameters bound the trajectory, matching clamped controls — [arXiv 2212.00541](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.00541)
- Performance: total planning time per update 1–20 ms on a single CPU for all examples; 27-DOF humanoid (21 actions) stands up from the floor and recovers from large disturbances; MuJoCo simulates that humanoid 4000x faster than real time on one thread — [arXiv 2212.00541](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.00541)
- Cost design: a weighted sum of terms, each a norm of a residual vector (with risk-sensitive transformation available) — [arXiv 2212.00541](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.00541)
- Key practical lessons: warm-starting "has a large beneficial effect"; planner and agent run asynchronously; planning with a model that mismatches the simulation is tolerated — [arXiv 2212.00541](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.00541)
- Limitations: "can only control what MuJoCo can simulate" much faster than real time, and MPC is myopic beyond its horizon; suggested fixes are learned policies as extra samples and learned terminal value functions — [arXiv 2212.00541](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.00541)

**MPPI and annealed variants on real legged robots (2024–2026) [CURRENT]**
- MPPI samples N control sequences, rolls them out, and updates the mean as an exponentially (temperature λ) weighted average of samples by cost — [Alvarez-Padilla et al., arXiv 2409.10469](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.10469)
- Hardware numbers (Unitree Go1): MPPI at 100 Hz, dynamics at 0.01 s, horizon 0.4 s (40 steps), only 30 rollouts, on an Intel i9-12900KS CPU; cost plateaus at about 40 samples for quadruped walking; authors claim the first whole-body sampling MPC on real legged hardware; published ICRA 2025, pp. 14721–14727 — [arXiv 2409.10469](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.10469), [CMU RI record](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/https-arxiv-org-pdf-2409-10469)
- Limitations stated: myopic beyond horizon; can only plan what the simulator can simulate — [arXiv 2409.10469](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.10469)
- DIAL-MPC (Xue et al., CMU, arXiv Sept 2024): MPPI plus diffusion-style annealing of sampling covariance over iterations and over the horizon; "training-free"; real-time 50 Hz on a quadruped; compared at N = 2048 samples, horizon 20 steps (0.4 s); claims 13.4x lower tracking error than vanilla MPPI and 50% better than RL on crate climbing; also shown on simulated humanoid jogging (19 actions). Limitation: needs fast simulation, restricting horizon — [arXiv 2409.15610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15610)
- Hybrid MPPI + CEM/CMA updates improve sample efficiency in quadruped simulation (Keshavarz et al., Aug 2025) — [arXiv 2508.11917](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11917)
- Judo (Li et al., June 2025, RSS 2025 workshop): open-source package with MPPI, CEM, predictive sampling and benchmark tasks, CPU-based — [arXiv 2506.17184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17184v1)
- Sampling-parameterisation study for legged MPPI (unstructured noise vs splines), Jan 2026 — [arXiv 2601.01409](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01409v1)

**iLQR whole-body MPC on hardware [CURRENT]**
- Zhang, Howell, Yi, Pan, Shi, Qu, Erez, Tassa, Manchester, "Whole-Body Model-Predictive Control of Legged Robots with MuJoCo" (arXiv March 2025; v3 March 2026; to appear ICRA 2026): iLQR with MuJoCo dynamics and finite-difference derivatives runs real time on quadruped locomotion, a quadruped walking on two legs, and full-size humanoid walking, with minimal sim-to-real effort — [arXiv 2503.04613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04613)
- Lineage: Erez et al., Humanoids 2013, real-time MPC on full humanoid dynamics enabled by MuJoCo — [Erez et al. 2013](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/%7Etodorov/papers/ErezHumanoids13.pdf); iLQG online MPC used a 0.5 s horizon for humanoid get-up — [Kwiatkowski et al. survey 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.04735)

**SAMCON (Liu, Yin, van de Panne, Shao, Xu; SIGGRAPH 2010) [HISTORICAL, influential] — read in full text**
- Open-loop control reconstruction by sampling: perturb PD target poses of a reference motion within per-joint sampling windows, simulate forward, keep good samples, advance in time — [SAMCON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-sampControl/2010-TOG-samcon.pdf)
- Parameters: nSample samples per iteration, nSave survivors; each survivor seeds nSample/nSave new samples. Sample step 0.1 s (10 Hz) with simulation step 0.0005 s. Reported runs: 1400 samples per iteration on an 80-core cluster (nSave = 200); harder motions nSample = 2000, nSave = 500 — [SAMCON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-sampControl/2010-TOG-samcon.pdf)
- Selection is deliberately not greedy: discard the worst 40% by cost, then keep survivors spread across the remaining cost range (favouring low cost), because greedily keeping the best can dead-end due to "nearsightedness" — [SAMCON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-sampControl/2010-TOG-samcon.pdf)
- Window size depends on data noise and joint role (active support joints need larger windows than free-swinging ones); one set of sampling parameters worked for all tested tasks, including contact-rich rolling — [SAMCON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-sampControl/2010-TOG-samcon.pdf)
- Result is open-loop and offline (minutes on a small cluster); edited controllers have "no guarantee" of working and are re-tested automatically — [SAMCON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-sampControl/2010-TOG-samcon.pdf)
- Follow-up with sample trees and feedback policies: Liu, Yin, Guo 2015 — [Samcon2 paper](https://www2.cs.sfu.ca/~kkyin/papers/Samcon2.pdf)

**Online sampling MPC in graphics (Hämäläinen et al. 2014, 2015) [HISTORICAL precursor of today's sampling MPC]**
- "Online Motion Synthesis Using Sequential Monte Carlo" (SIGGRAPH 2014, TOG 33(4) 51): model-predictive control for a 36-DOF human that balances, dodges projectiles and gets up, with no offline precomputation, state machine or motion data — [project page](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC)
- "Online Control of Simulated Humanoids Using Particle Belief Propagation" (C-PBP, SIGGRAPH 2015, with C. K. Liu): multimodal gradient-free sampling with a Markov-random-field factorisation; shows balancing, recovery from extreme disturbances, reaching, balancing on a ball, juggling a ball, steerable locomotion at interactive rates, from simple cost functions — [project page](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/C-PBP/)

**Contact-Invariant Optimisation (Mordatch, Todorov, Popović; SIGGRAPH 2012) [HISTORICAL, concept current] — read in part**
- Adds per-end-effector, per-phase auxiliary contact variables so the optimiser chooses contacts continuously; the trajectory is split into K phases (number and duration predefined) with invariant contact set per phase — [CIO paper](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/behavior-discovery.pdf)
- Continuation schedule: optimisation runs in stages that weight task, physics and contact costs differently (Phase 1 task only), 250–1000 iterations per stage; total 2–10 minutes per clip on a quad-core 2.3 GHz Xeon; the same schedule worked for all behaviours — [CIO paper](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/behavior-discovery.pdf)
- Strikes are specified directly as targets: a strike task gives end-effector positions and velocities at set times (kicks: a random target every third phase), including velocity dependence "because we want the targets to be hit with a certain" velocity — [CIO paper](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/behavior-discovery.pdf)
- Limitations: simplified dynamics give effectively infinitely strong limbs and energy-inefficient poses; underestimating the number of phases is problematic, overestimating is harmless — [CIO paper](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/behavior-discovery.pdf)
- Later ensemble-CIO still took around 5 minutes of CPU time for walking and did not run in real time — [Mordatch, Lowrey, Todorov ICRA 2015](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/%7Etodorov/papers/MordatchICRA15.pdf)

### Inferences
- Real-time budget for the author's case: a planar figure with roughly 10–15 point masses is orders of magnitude cheaper per step than the 27-DOF MuJoCo humanoid, but the library's 1/1440 s step is expensive per simulated second (1440 steps). 30–40 rollouts of a 0.5 s horizon = roughly 22k–29k steps per plan. That is plausible per action (one-off rehearsal before a kick) in a browser, and plausible per frame only if the step can be raised (see Stable PD) or the plan is refreshed at 10–20 Hz with warm start, as in MJPC's asynchronous design.
- Replacing 1-D bisection, in increasing order of cost and robustness:
  1. Residual least squares: define r(p) = [speed − target_speed, angle − target_angle, ...] over parameters p (power, timing, lean, ...). Finite-difference Jacobian (one extra rehearsal per parameter), Gauss-Newton/Levenberg-Marquardt step, 3–6 iterations. Handles speed AND angle; fails on non-smooth or non-monotonic response.
  2. Sampling (predictive-sampling/CEM/CMA-ES over p): N rehearsals per iteration around the current best, keep the best or re-fit a Gaussian to the elite. Insensitive to non-monotonicity and discontinuities from contact, trivially parallel conceptually, 2 tunables (N, σ). This is the direct analogue of what MJPC found "surprisingly competitive".
  3. Coarse scan then local refinement: grid/Sobol sample the parameter box first (this is what detects non-monotonic response and multiple solutions), then refine the best basin with 1 or 2.
- Infeasibility reporting: none of these methods proves infeasibility (Tedrake's caveat). The honest contract is to return the best residual found with a status: "converged within tolerance", "bracket not found / target outside sampled outcome range", or "budget exhausted". A coarse scan gives an achievable-outcome range (e.g. max ball speed reached = 24 m/s), which converts the current silent failure into an explicit "27 m/s is outside what this figure can do; best = 24 m/s".
- For bisection specifically: check monotonicity on the bracket by sampling interior points before bisecting, and verify the final rehearsal's residual; a non-bracketing or non-monotone scan should fall back to sampling.
- SAMCON's non-greedy survivor selection is a cheap, transferable trick when chaining segments (wind-up, strike, follow-through): keep several diverse candidates per segment rather than the single best.
- CIO, collocation and iLQR need differentiable or smoothed contact and derivative code; for a PBD simulator without analytic derivatives they are poor value compared with sampling. iLQR with finite differences (as in the 2025 MuJoCo work) is feasible for low DOF but costs 2·(nx + nu) extra steps per timestep per iteration.
- Determinism is a prerequisite: rehearsal-based targeting only works if the cloned simulation reproduces the real one bit-for-bit (fixed step, no wall-clock dependence).

### Gaps
- CMA-ES itself (Hansen) and its use in graphics (e.g. Wang et al. 2009/2010 optimising SIMBICON-style controllers, Al Borno et al. 2013 full-body trajectory optimisation, Geijtenbeek et al. 2013) were not verified against primary sources in this pass; only a search snippet confirmed Wang et al. optimised SIMBICON's free parameters. Population sizes and iteration counts are therefore not given.
- No source found that benchmarks sampling MPC in JavaScript/WebAssembly or on a planar stick figure; the real-time judgement above is inference from CPU-native results.
- Witkin & Kass 1988 "Spacetime Constraints" was not fetched; its formulation is represented here by the direct transcription description in Tedrake's notes.
- I did not find a published treatment of "report infeasibility" for outcome-targeted character actions; the proposal above is engineering inference.
- The quantitative iLQG vs predictive sampling comparison in the MJPC paper was not extracted beyond the "surprisingly competitive" statement.

## 4. Whole-body control: QP controllers, momentum-based control, feature-based control

### Takeaway
Whole-body control computes joint torques each frame by solving a small optimisation (usually a QP) over accelerations, torques and contact forces so that task-space objectives (CoM, angular momentum, end-effectors) are met subject to dynamics, friction and torque limits. In graphics its peak was 2009–2010 (Macchietto; de Lasa); in robotics it remains the standard low-level layer under MPC. For a planar point-foot stick figure the full QP is usually overkill; Jacobian-transpose virtual forces capture most of the benefit.

### Cited Findings
- Centroidal dynamics are exact, not an approximation: total spatial momentum L = A(q)·q̇ (A = centroidal momentum matrix) and A(q)·q̈ + Ȧ·q̇ = Σ external wrenches; the simplification lies only in ignoring kinematic and actuator limits — [Tedrake ch. 5](https://underactuated.mit.edu/humanoids.html)
- Standard robotics reference for the QP layer: Kuindersma et al., "An Efficiently Solvable Quadratic Program for Stabilizing Dynamic Locomotion" (ICRA 2014), used on Atlas; Tedrake's notes point to it and to Kuindersma et al. 2016 for whole-body control and to Koolen 2012 for push-recovery geometry (the notes' own QP section is marked "coming soon") — [Tedrake ch. 5](https://underactuated.mit.edu/humanoids.html)
- Macchietto, Zordan, Shelton, "Momentum Control for Balance" (SIGGRAPH 2009, ACM TOG 28(3) art. 80): standing balance by controlling linear and angular momentum via an abstract CoM/momentum objective realised through full-body optimisation — bibliographic and one-line characterisation from [a later paper's related work](https://graphics.cs.ucr.edu/projects/rotationControl/brown:2013:ROT.pdf)
- de Lasa, Mordatch, Hertzmann, "Feature-Based Locomotion Controllers" (SIGGRAPH 2010, ACM TOG 29(4), published 26 July 2010, DOI 10.1145/1778765.1781157): control via high-level features — centre of mass, angular momentum, end-effectors — with a prioritisation algorithm; no motion capture or offline optimisation; keywords include standing jump and balance — [SIGGRAPH history archive](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=105853), [Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145%2F1778765.1781157)
- de Lasa's thesis applies the approach to biped balancing, jumping, walking and jogging; the prioritised solver comes from de Lasa & Hertzmann, "Prioritized Optimization for Task-Space Control" (IROS 2009) — [thesis record](https://openaccess.library.uitm.edu.my/Record/ndltd-LACETR-oai-collectionscanada.gc.ca-OTU.1807-29923/Description)
- Lightweight alternative: Jacobian-transpose virtual forces (gravity compensation plus CoM velocity force) achieved 600 N push robustness in GBWC without any QP — [GBWC paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Robotics practice: a whole-body locomotion controller paired with a simple foot-placement planner (p = kp·x + kd·ẋ form) on passive-ankle bipeds — [Kim et al., arXiv 1901.08100](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.08100)
- Industry robotics: Boston Dynamics describes Atlas's controller as a model-predictive controller; earlier parkour/dance used MPC "with a very simple model" (total CoM and inertia); the 2025 Large Behavior Model work is built on top of the MPC system, which is also used for teleoperation data collection, with the learned policy outputting actions at 30 Hz — [Boston Dynamics LBM blog](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/large-behavior-models-atlas-find-new-footing/)
- Boston Dynamics on Spot: a hybrid where the model-based locomotion controller handles what it does well and RL handles the harder part — [Boston Dynamics RL blog](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/starting-on-the-right-foot-with-reinforcement-learning/)
- Games: as of the 2012 state-of-the-art review, commercial applications "still resort to kinematics-based approaches" for actively controlled characters despite research progress in physics-based control — [Geijtenbeek & Pronost, CGF 31(8):2492–2515, 2012](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/v31i8pp2492-2515)
- Still true in 2025 for sports games: football titles use a kinematic character with a physics-based ball, per the PhysicsFC authors' SIGGRAPH 2025 presentation — [PhysicsFC slides](https://gitcgr.hanyang.ac.kr/publications/2025-physicsfc/physicsfc-siggraph-presentation.pdf)

### Inferences
- A planar figure makes momentum control tractable without a QP: total linear momentum is Σ mi·vi, angular momentum about the CoM is Σ mi·(ri − c) × vi (a scalar in 2D), both trivially computed from point masses. A feature-based controller can be: desired CoM acceleration (PD on CoM over support) and desired angular-momentum rate (PD to zero), converted to a desired ground reaction force and realised with Jacobian-transpose torques through the stance leg.
- The feature list from de Lasa (CoM, angular momentum, end-effectors) is the right abstraction for outcome-targeted actions: a kick is "foot end-effector velocity target at time t while CoM stays over the stance foot and angular momentum is bounded"; arms and torso become the null-space resource that cancels angular momentum.
- A full QP (decision variables: joint accelerations, torques, contact forces; constraints: dynamics, friction cone, torque limits) is small in 2D (tens of variables) and solvable at frame rate, but requires an articulated-body mass matrix the point-mass/rod PBD formulation does not expose; this is the main adoption barrier, not compute.
- The pattern "reduced-model planner (LIPM/capture point) above, whole-body tracker below" is the stable architecture across graphics (GBWC), robotics (WBLC, Atlas) and should guide the library's layering: foot placement from CoM state; torques from task-space forces plus PD.

### Gaps
- Macchietto 2009 and de Lasa 2010 full texts were not read; no equations from them are given (only bibliographic facts and abstract-level descriptions). The exact objective weights and prioritisation scheme are unverified.
- No primary source obtained on what shipped games use for active physical characters (e.g. NaturalMotion Euphoria internals); public technical detail is scarce and I found none in this pass.
- The Kuindersma 2014 QP formulation was not extracted; Tedrake's chapter section on it is unfinished.
- The Boston Dynamics split between MPC and RL in the current Atlas stack is described only in company blog posts, not a peer-reviewed architecture paper.

## 5. Research status 2024–2026: model-based versus learned

### Takeaway
In character animation research, deep RL with motion imitation has been the dominant paradigm since about 2018 and I found no 2025–2026 SIGGRAPH character paper that is purely model-based; classical controllers survive as baselines, components and teachers. In robotics, by contrast, model-based control had a visible revival in 2024–2026 through training-free sampling MPC and simple iLQR whole-body MPC on real hardware, and the active frontier is hybridisation (RL policy as sampling prior, MPC as data source or safety/precision layer).

### Cited Findings
- The 2012 state-of-the-art review covers two decades of pre-learning physics-based character control and is the reference survey for the classical era (Geijtenbeek & Pronost, CGF 31(8), DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03189.x; extended from a Eurographics 2011 STAR) — [Eurographics DL](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/v31i8pp2492-2515)
- The most recent broad character-animation survey I found is RL-centred: Kwiatkowski et al., "A Survey on Reinforcement Learning Methods in Character Animation" (Eurographics STAR, CGF 41(2), 2022). It treats MPC as "a form of model-based RL", notes MPC methods "have been successfully employed for humanoid animation", cites iLQG with a 0.5 s horizon and the Hämäläinen sampling methods, and describes trajectory optimisation as a guide/bootstrap for policy learning — [arXiv 2203.04735](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.04735)
- SIGGRAPH 2025 physics-based character papers surfaced by search are learning-based (e.g. PARC for terrain traversal, AMOR for tracking with RL) — [SIGGRAPH 2025 conference papers list](https://www.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Conference-Papers.html)
- Robotics revival, training-free: predictive sampling (2022) — [arXiv 2212.00541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00541); MPPI whole-body on real quadruped with 30 samples (ICRA 2025) — [arXiv 2409.10469](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.10469); DIAL-MPC claims to be the first training-free method to match/beat RL on certain agile tasks and stresses instant redeployment when the model changes, whereas an RL baseline failed with a 10 kg payload — [arXiv 2409.15610](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.15610)
- Robotics revival, gradient-based: iLQR + MuJoCo + finite differences as a deliberately simple reproducible hardware baseline for quadrupeds and a full-size humanoid (to appear ICRA 2026) — [arXiv 2503.04613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04613)
- Hybrid, RL inside MPC: "RGB: RL Guided Whole-Body MPPI for Humanoid Control" (arXiv 23 June 2026): a pretrained RL policy acts as the sampling prior for MPPI; task objectives are added as modular cost terms without retraining; improves task precision over the pure RL baseline and corrects walking drift; average 280 Hz in MuJoCo on a 29-DoF Unitree G1 (simulation) — [arXiv 2606.25123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25123)
- Hybrid, MPC guiding RL: "Accelerating and Scaling MPC-Guided Reinforcement Learning for Humanoid Locomotion and Manipulation" (arXiv June 2026) — title-level only, not read — [arXiv 2606.05687](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05687)
- MPC as teacher: a December 2025 mini-review of imitation learning for legged locomotion (35 works) finds MPC logs are the most common demonstration source (11 papers, 31%), "scalable, noise-free, albeit domain-limited"; a distilled policy "cannot outperform MPC for the same cost function and cannot learn in the areas where MPC does not converge"; MPC distillation cut compute 20x and enabled 1 kHz control on ANYmal — [Mirza & Singh, Frontiers in Robotics and AI 2025, DOI 10.3389/frobt.2025.1678567](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2025.1678567/full)
- The MJPC authors themselves frame the path forward as hybrid: learned policies as extra rollout candidates and learned value functions as terminal costs, "AlphaGo for control" — [arXiv 2212.00541](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.00541)
- Tooling signal: Judo (2025) packages MPPI/CEM/predictive sampling with benchmarks, indicating sampling MPC has become a commodity baseline — [arXiv 2506.17184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17184v1)
- Industry: Atlas remains MPC-based at the control layer with learned behaviour models above it (2025) — [Boston Dynamics blog](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/large-behavior-models-atlas-find-new-footing/)

### Inferences
- Where model-based is still preferred in 2026: (a) no training budget or data, and the model changes often (new character proportions, new props) — exactly a small library's situation; (b) precise, explicitly specified objectives (hit 27 m/s at 30°) that are awkward to bake into a policy; (c) interpretability and debuggability; (d) low-DOF systems where reduced models are accurate. Learned controllers win on long-horizon robustness, naturalness from mocap, and amortised runtime cost.
- For a browser-run 2D stick figure, the 2024–2026 robotics lesson transfers directly: a few dozen rollouts of a short horizon with warm start is a credible real-time controller when the simulator is fast and deterministic. The library's "rehearse on a cloned state" routine is already the core primitive of sampling MPC.
- The hybrid pattern "cheap prior proposes, sampling refines against an explicit cost" can be used without any learning: the hand-scripted controller (current scripted keyframes/PD targets) plays the role of the prior, and sampling perturbs its parameters. This keeps the authored look while fixing targeting and brittleness.
- The absence of new purely classical character-animation papers at SIGGRAPH does not mean the methods are obsolete for engineering; it means they are considered solved/baseline for research novelty purposes.

### Gaps
- I found no 2023–2026 survey dedicated to classical/model-based physics-based character animation, and no survey that directly compares model-based with learned character controllers; the status assessment is assembled from individual papers.

## 6. Athletic skills in physics-based animation: kicking, throwing, jumping, striking

### Takeaway
Published athletic skills split into three eras: hand-built state machines with control laws (Hodgins et al. 1995), offline trajectory optimisation with momentum or contact reasoning (Liu & Popović 2002; Abe et al. 2004; Mordatch et al. 2012 — which specified kicks and punches as timed end-effector position-and-velocity targets), and RL-based skill learning (2018 onward: basketball, high jump, soccer juggling, football). Classical real-time controllers for targeted kicks or throws with a specified ball velocity are rare in the literature; the nearest are feature-based jumping (de Lasa 2010) and online sampling MPC that juggles a ball (Hämäläinen 2015).

### Cited Findings
- Hodgins, Wooten, Brogan, O'Brien, "Animating Human Athletics" (SIGGRAPH 1995, pp. 71–78): hand-designed control algorithms on a dynamic human model for running, bicycling and a handspring vault; state machines sequence phases; results compared with video and biomechanical data. Companion work: Wooten & Hodgins, "Simulation of Human Diving" (Graphics Interface 1995) — [CMU RI record](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/animating-human-athletics), [SIGGRAPH history archive](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=118721)
- Liu & Popović, "Synthesis of Complex Dynamic Character Motion from Simple Animations" (SIGGRAPH 2002): targets "highly dynamic movement such as jumping, kicking, running, and gymnastics"; iterative offline optimisation turns rough keyframes into physically plausible motion (example: karate kick) — [project page](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/charanim/phylter.html)
- Abe, Liu, Popović, "Momentum-based Parameterization of Dynamic Character Motion" (SCA 2004): momentum-based editing of running, leaping, jumping and acrobatic manoeuvres — [paper copy](https://typeset.io/pdf/momentum-based-parameterization-of-dynamic-character-motion-3kultp429c.pdf)
- de Lasa et al. 2010: standing jump generated from feature objectives (CoM, angular momentum, end-effectors) with no mocap or offline optimisation — [SIGGRAPH history archive](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=105853)
- Mordatch et al. 2012 (CIO): punches and kicks via a "strike" task giving limb end-effector target positions and velocities at set times; handstands via an orientation task; props can be included as optimised objects with their own contact variables; 2–10 minutes offline per clip — [CIO paper](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/behavior-discovery.pdf)
- SAMCON 2010: reconstructs open-loop control for contact-rich mocap, including rolling motions not handled by earlier reconstruction methods, with a single parameter set (the paper text does not mention kicks) — [SAMCON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-sampControl/2010-TOG-samcon.pdf)
- Hämäläinen, Rajamäki, Liu 2015 (C-PBP): online, data-free sampling MPC that balances on a ball and juggles a ball at interactive rates — [project page](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/C-PBP/)
- Tan, Liu, Turk 2011 use catching a basketball as the example of a timing-critical motion that over-damped implicit integration would spoil — [SPD paper](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf)
- Liu & Hodgins, basketball dribbling (SIGGRAPH 2018): trajectory optimisation recovers plausible ball paths from hand mocap, then deep RL learns control in two stages (locomotion, then arms/hands); authors note the decoupled approach suits dribbling/juggling where the object does not affect balance, while soccer is harder because balance couples with ball handling — [CMU RI news](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/?p=107195)
- Yin, Yang, van de Panne, Yin, "Discovering Diverse Athletic Jumping Strategies" (SIGGRAPH 2021): physics simulation plus deep RL discovers strategies for skills such as the high jump — [SIGGRAPH history archive](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=121206)
- Xie et al., "Learning Soccer Juggling Skills with Layer-wise Mixture-of-Experts" (SIGGRAPH 2022): juggling with foot, chest and head via a control graph and RL — [paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2022-SIGGRAPH-juggle/soccer_juggling.pdf)
- PhysicsFC (SIGGRAPH 2025; arXiv April 2025): user-controlled physically simulated football player with moving, trapping, dribbling and kicking and transitions between them, trained on top of a physics-based motion embedding of football mocap — [arXiv 2504.21216](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21216v2)
- Jump-related robustness in classical controllers: SIMBICON covers running, skipping and hopping gaits with flight phases from 2-state machines — [SIMBICON paper](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2007-siggraph-simbicon.pdf)

### Inferences
- The classical recipe for a targeted strike, assembled from the above: (1) reduce the outcome to an end-effector condition at contact time — CIO's strike task is literally "end-effector position and velocity at time t"; for a ball of mass m_b and foot effective mass m_f with restitution e, ball exit speed is approximately v_foot·(1 + e)·m_f/(m_f + m_b), so the target becomes a foot velocity vector; (2) plan a wind-up/swing with a proximal-to-distal sequence of PD targets or a spline; (3) hold balance with CoM-over-support and angular-momentum compensation from arms/torso (feature-based control); (4) close the loop on the outcome by rehearsal-based sampling over a few parameters (swing power, timing, stance offset, lean). Steps 1 and 4 are what the library's bisection approximates with one parameter.
- For jumps the outcome is ballistic and analytic: take-off CoM velocity (vx, vy) fixes apex height vy²/(2g) and range, so targeting reduces to achieving a take-off momentum, which a feature-based CoM-acceleration objective during the push-off phase handles; angular momentum at take-off fixes rotation in flight (conserved), which is why momentum is the right control feature.
- Throws are the same structure as kicks with the release time as an extra parameter; speed and angle are two outcomes, so at least two parameters must be searched — a 1-D bisection cannot hit both except by luck.
- Non-monotonic response is expected physically in kicks (more power can disturb balance, change contact timing or contact point, reducing ball speed), so the search method must tolerate it; this is evidence for sampling-based or scan-then-refine targeting.
- The literature gap (few classical real-time targeted-kick controllers) means the author's design is not reinventing a solved textbook method; the closest published templates are CIO's strike objective (offline) and sampling MPC with a cost on ball state (online).
